\paragraph{Draft status.} Reference qualification and neural training are complete; held-out inverse fits, baseline comparisons, and acquisition evaluation are in progress. Prediction results will be reported for complete declared cohorts.

\paragraph{Geometry and observations.}
The controlled object is a $240\times80\times80$\,mm deformable block with known canonical geometry, density $625$\,kg/m$^3$, and Poisson ratio $0.35$. Two position-controlled tools couple to unknown smooth Gaussian attachment footprints. The observed prefix contains three settled configurations: nominal tool poses, a 6\,mm right-tool translation along $y$, and a 6\,mm translation along $z$. A $640\times480$ rendered RGB-D marker pipeline provides the same 27 visible point positions to all methods. Known synthetic marker IDs establish correspondence; depth has independent 0.3\,mm Gaussian noise and image sampling introduces pixel quantization. The positional objective uses a common 0.3\,mm coordinate scale. No force, reaction, material, or attachment labels are supplied. Predictions are evaluated at 81 canonical query points, with visible and hidden-surface subsets reported separately.

\paragraph{Cohorts and shifts.}
The protocol declares nine development objects, twelve calibration objects, and three disjoint test cohorts: eighteen objects from the development parameter ranges, twelve with extrapolated elastic coefficients and spatial heterogeneity, and twelve with shifted environmental-field amplitude, center, width, and gravity direction. Material coefficients within development range from 8 to 40\,kPa, and total attachment stiffnesses from 1500 to 15000\,N/m. Each object has a new 3\,mm familiar-direction query, five new positional interaction patterns, and a doubled-amplitude query. New patterns include stretching, opposed tool motion, twisting, bending, and diagonal translation. An additional transfer challenge translates both tools together through the fixed spatial field. These are declared interaction and parameter shifts; the reference material law remains Neo-Hookean.

\paragraph{Unseen environmental force laws.}
A separately declared six-object extension tests anisotropic harmonic fields and signed two-Gaussian fields, with three objects per law and one per attachment layout. Both laws lie outside the single-Gaussian environmental family used by the inverse predictor. Each object supplies the same three prefix observations and is queried with the ten local actions and six whole-object translations. Materials, gravity direction, and compliant coupling remain unknown. All six predictors retain their frozen settings and learned weights; extension results are reported separately from the primary cohorts. All six reference fields pass the original numerical qualification criteria. The supplement gives the potentials and protocol.

\paragraph{Numerical references.}
Reference geometry uses quadratic finite elements and independent spatial and quadrature refinement. Every action must have a displacement RMS change below 20\,$\mu$m, equilibrium residual below $10^{-9}$\,N, positive deformation Jacobians above $0.1$, and reaction balance below $10^{-7}$\,N. Maximum-coordinate refinement changes are retained separately, since localized attachment-edge behavior converges more slowly. Acceptance in an RMS norm does not certify pointwise stress convergence. The inverse predictor uses a smaller discretization and an independently checked dense tangent implementation. A development-only sharp-attachment pilot motivated the finite smooth footprint: its localized compression reached the Jacobian guard under refinement, whereas the smooth version retained substantial nonrigid deformation and passed the original numerical thresholds.

\paragraph{Deformation content.}
All 87 reference fields pass the declared criteria: 63 independent development, calibration, and test objects, plus 24 spatial-transfer fields that reuse their physical parameters. The largest spatial-refinement RMS change is 19.34\,$\mu$m, the largest quadrature-refinement change is 0.278\,$\mu$m, and the smallest deformation Jacobian is 0.727. These checks qualify the displacement references used for evaluation.

Across all 42 test objects, the median object-average nonrigid coordinate RMS over the five new interaction patterns is 4.64\,mm, with an object range of 2.24--12.38\,mm. Relative to the already-deformed nominal configuration, the median nonrigid change induced by those actions is 0.89\,mm (range 0.64--1.19\,mm), after removing the best rigid transform between configurations. The benchmark therefore contains substantial shape change at both the configuration and interaction levels.

\paragraph{Comparisons.}
The physical baselines use official PhysTwin spring-mass and PhysGaussian material-point simulation components with documented adapters for the shared positional interface. They retain their own constitutive representations. PhysTwin learns per-spring parameters; the MPM adapter fits material variation, compliant tool coupling, and gravity direction. We tune initialization, discretization, and settling on development cases. A positional neural predictor is trained on a separate synthetic object bank, with an ID-action training variant and a separately reported variant exposed to all local action families. Controlled ablations fix environmental loading to its nominal value or freeze the spatial force at each material point's canonical position. The main predictor and neural comparisons cover all 42 declared test objects. The more expensive physical comparisons use the first six objects in each test cohort, giving two objects per attachment layout and an 18-object matched subset selected before test inference. Full Gaussian appearance reconstruction is outside this component benchmark.

\paragraph{Metrics and adaptation.}
The primary error is coordinate RMS displacement error,
\begin{equation}
 e=\sqrt{\frac{1}{3N}\sum_{i=1}^N\|\hat u_i-u_i\|_2^2}.
\end{equation}
We also report Euclidean point RMSE, hidden-surface error, and residual motion after removing the best rigid transform. Incremental error compares $\hat u(a)-\hat u(a_0)$ against $u(a)-u(a_0)$, separating the predicted response to the new action from static shape-completion error. Aggregation treats the object as the independent unit. Paired comparisons resample objects rather than frames or points. Probing compares a fixed $+40$\,mm $y$ translation, a reproducible random action, and the sensitivity-based policy, all with one additional acquired response and a separate $-40$\,mm $y$ target. Twelve independent calibration objects set separate pre- and post-acquisition interval scales; the first nine familiar-parameter test objects evaluate transfer. A material-indexed-load ablation receives exactly the probe response selected for the spatial model. Before acquisition, the two neural baselines also predict all nine transfer objects; physical baselines reuse their original fits on the first six objects, with no additional observed responses.

\section{Prediction Results}
\paragraph{New interactions with familiar parameters.}
On all eighteen familiar-parameter objects, the spatial inverse predicts the five new local motions with mean coordinate RMSE of $0.068$\,mm. The all-action neural baseline gives $0.119$\,mm and the ID-action baseline $1.528$\,mm. Relative to all-action training, the inverse reduces mean error by $43.1\%$, with paired advantage $0.051$\,mm and 95\% object-bootstrap interval $[0.037,0.066]$\,mm. The all-action model has seen these interaction families on disjoint training objects, making it a useful comparison for the benefit of per-object physical inference beyond action-family exposure.

\paragraph{Environmental-shift generalization.}
On all twelve environmental-shift objects, the spatial inverse has new-local-motion coordinate RMSE of $0.067$\,mm, compared with $0.216$\,mm for the neural predictor trained on all local action families and $1.488$\,mm for ID-action training. Relative to the all-action baseline, the mean error is reduced by $68.9\%$; the paired mean advantage is $0.149$\,mm with a 95\% object-bootstrap interval of $[0.066,0.276]$\,mm. The spatial inverse also gives $0.059$\,mm on the familiar-direction query and $0.073$\,mm at doubled amplitude. These results use the original three observations and frozen model settings. The material-shift cohort, physical baselines and acquisition comparisons are still being evaluated.

With the same fixed calibration scale, predictive intervals on the eighteen familiar-parameter objects cover $98.3\%$ of held-action coordinates on average, with mean half-width $0.169$\,mm; all eighteen objects reach $90\%$ coordinate coverage.

On the environmental-shift cohort, the independently calibrated predictive intervals cover $98.7\%$ of held-action coordinates on average in this cohort, with mean half-width $0.181$\,mm; all twelve objects have at least $90\%$ coordinate coverage. These measurements pool the seven held actions per object and retain the calibration scale fixed on the twelve calibration objects. They assess predictive coverage before acquisition, separately from physical-parameter identifiability and post-probe performance.
