# C38: implementation checks and remaining mesh errors

The faster Warp implementation passes a strict paired impact-trajectory check after tightening both integrator runs. This is a useful implementation result. The difficult cantilever remains spatially unqualified, and the varied supported-block dataset is not admitted. Learned prediction and uncertainty-guided action advantages remain unproved.

The original paired six-tetrahedron run reached its 300-second cap after completing Torch but before completing Warp. A separate Warp-only phase reused the completed Torch result, retaining the recomputed partial-prefix cost. At the original 1e-9 relative tolerance, both implementations conserved energy and momentum, but their trajectories differed by 21.96 nm and 29.02 µm/s, failing the prospective implementation limits of 10 nm and 10 µm/s. Same-state force parity alone did not qualify trajectory agreement.

A new pair used 1e-10 relative tolerance, displacement/velocity absolute tolerances 1e-13 m and 1e-11 m/s, and the same maximum step 1/30000 s. Both used the same six-tet model, full consistent mass, Q8 contact rule, finite plane stiffness, initial condition and output clocks. The maximum position difference is **7.452 nm**, and the maximum velocity difference is **7.852 µm/s**: both original limits pass. Torch took 391.665 s and Warp 156.021 s, including their respective per-case work. Their maximum energy errors are 9.920 and 4.993 nJ; momentum, positive-J and mass-solve checks also pass. This roughly 2.51-fold per-case speedup is specific to this workload. The full two-case phase took 551.55 s.

The earlier loose-tolerance negative remains. Tightening reduced the observed disagreement, consistent with adaptive integration error contributing to it; it does not establish an error bound at other meshes. The next step is a separate 96-tet Q8 Warp bridge against the completed tight Torch reference, followed by a same-backend contact-surface quadrature comparison. Neither a six-tet backend check nor an equal-state force benchmark qualifies contact spatial convergence, a rigid-contact limit or dynamic training data.

The 393,216-tet graded cantilever solve reached its 40-minute limit inside a linear solve. It left no converged field for a finer comparison; the previously reported 25.591 µm spatial-indicator failure remains. No nonexistent field is used as a warm start. Its approximately 61.6% peak strain on the preceding mesh also prevents describing it as a qualified moderate-strain dataset.

A new graded 8×4×4 coarse preconditioner was tested against the old uniform 4×2×2 correction on the **same** saved 165,888-tet field and exact polynomial bulk linear system. The physical equations and original residual gates were unchanged. Both corrections agree to 1.06e-13 m, and both corrected fields agree with the saved exact80 equilibrium within 1e-13 m. The new preconditioner reduces CG iterations from 270 to 224 and linear-solve time from 113.50 to 94.97 s. However, initialization grows from 13.00 to 40.00 s, making total case time **138.40 s versus 129.96 s**, about 6.5% slower. This is a verified numerical alternative, not an overall speed improvement on this benchmark. A large cantilever retry is parked while the more informative supported-block work proceeds.

A separate 14-case prospective phase tests 75–150 kPa, two fixed support layouts and three public midspan-force values under gravity. Its source, force compiler, protocol and preparation controls accompany this update, but this publication does not assert that the family passes. Both layouts use the same geometry and loads, compiled without material/support inputs. Every accepted Newton iterate is checkpointed. Physical failures stop new cases; completed spatial failures remain in the matrix. No object roles, observations, images or model fits are admitted by those preparation artifacts.

A [prospective reference-inference design](GAUSSIAN_C38_SUPPORTED_REFERENCE_DESIGN.md) separates discrete support hypotheses from continuous stiffness and requires forward, likelihood and integration convergence before learning another posterior. A source-only static public-data API represents equilibrium acquisitions by integer order and known load-state indices. Its four tests reject hidden material/support/full-state members and future acquisitions before image reads. It creates no dataset and does not replace physics or renderer qualification. Static held-load prediction remains distinct from future-motion prediction.

Raw simulation arrays, images, videos and model checkpoints remain local. All capped runs and failed checks are retained. The larger prediction and active-probing campaign continues.
