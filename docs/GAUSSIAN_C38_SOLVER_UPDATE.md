# Faster solves and a concrete contact diagnosis

The new elastic Hessian-vector kernel passed a paired GPU control on the same 165,888-tetrahedron linear problem. The original Torch solve took 150.727 seconds and the Warp solve took 9.811 seconds, with corrections differing by at most 2.19e-14 m. Both corrections passed the independent original Torch operator and final gradient checks. JIT took another 7.808 seconds and is reported separately. This is one linear-solve comparison, not a measured full nonlinear or trajectory speedup. The [complete control](../runs/operator-dev/p2-torch-warp-hvp-benchmark-v02/result-summary.json) retains the earlier pre-CG failure caused by an unnecessary bitwise comparison of two floating-point gradient evaluations.

The remaining static family checks use that frozen kernel with an independent original Torch operator check on every returned CG correction. They retain the original constitutive law, constraints, forces, final 24/80-point residual checks, whole-element Jacobian checks and 20 µm conditional displacement indicator. Exactly four completed fine fields are reused; the remaining twelve cases require 24 new solves from rest. All 14 original coarse controls have matching physical arrays and full mass matrices. Auxiliary cache and duplicate guard storage differences are explicitly recorded. The [preparation](../runs/operator-dev/c38-graded-family-warp-v01/protocol-v01.json) is source and protocol evidence; it contains no new family outcome or admission.

The contact error is now visible at the same frozen geometry, independently of trajectory differences. At the saved 0.108333 s state, the Q8 contact force differs from a root-split adaptive CPU integration by 0.236229 N in vector norm; Q16 still differs by 0.035281 N. Near 0.102 s those errors are 0.231995 N and 0.090637 N. This isolates a surface integration error in the unchanged finite compliant plane law. It does not qualify the saved Q16 trajectory as truth.

The proposed replacement finds where each quadratic surface actually crosses the plane, integrates the active intervals exactly in one coordinate, and resolves the remaining integral separately. CPU controls retain two negative findings: the coarse outer rule failed a Hessian tolerance, and a ten-times tighter adaptive calculation hit a roundoff limit. The finer fixed rule agrees with the successful adaptive reference at the four declared saved states. Those are fixed-state controls, not a contact trajectory, spatial convergence or hard-contact result. Geometry-dependent quadrature nodes also require an energy-to-force finite-difference check; algebraic discrete conservation is not assumed.

The four gravity trajectory comparisons remain separate space and time checks. Static equilibrium, recorded trajectory positions, velocities and peak stress have different gates. A conditional spatial indicator is not a rigorous error bound, and separate passing indicators would not establish a combined 20 µm bound.

No new observations, cohort, training run, material posterior or active-policy advantage is admitted here.
