Editable scene programme, first integration milestone (v23)

Target: infer persistent objects, appearance, material and interaction hypotheses
from an observed prefix; edit tool positions, force inputs, gravity and supports;
forecast dynamics and render from chosen cameras; quantify predictive uncertainty.

Implemented first milestone
- Explicit SI material hypothesis and intervention contracts, separate cameras.
- Existing coupled dynamic FEM backend reused; prescribed lift/release, free drop,
  localized force pulse, gravity and floor contact. Analytic material in this pilot.
- Force spatial weights sum to one. External potential and time-dependent power
  use the same applied load. Camera transforms never enter dynamics.
- Mixed-supervision helpers preserve a causal observation prefix, allow known
  future actions, and mask unavailable labels before computing gradients.
- Ten controlled rollouts: three material hypotheses x three interventions plus
  one timestep-halving run. These are validation of wiring, not learned inference.
- Pinned PhysTwin release inventory and resumable three-worker full-RGB download.
  Raw data stays local. Deform360 remains paused and PokeFlex access unresolved.

Next implementation stages, not yet complete
1. Verify full-video acquisition, obtain corresponding depth/tracks/calibration,
   group identical objects before allocating training/validation/test splits.
   The previously exposed eight plush sequences remain development-only.
2. Train a shared video/geometry query encoder with available RGB, depth, camera,
   visibility and 3D-track labels. Use an available pretrained backbone; do not
   claim a reproduction of D4RT without author weights/protocol.
3. Add persistent object/material/interaction tokens and action-conditioned dynamic
   predictions; joint training with short rollouts, then longer held-action tests.
   Force and position actuation are separate inputs; unsupported labels masked.
4. Fit Gaussian appearance on training cameras only; evaluate held-out recorded
   cameras separately from future times and novel action families.
5. Train multiple physical hypotheses, calibrate predictive intervals on reserved
   objects/actions, report coverage plus interval width and OOD failures. Current
   three-material sensitivity envelope is not a posterior or confidence interval.
6. Reproduce PIDG's reconstruction task, compare with matched inputs, then disclose
   any action-conditioned PIDG adaptation separately. Preserve current benchmarks.
7. Evaluate view/poke selection only after uncertainty calibration, against random
   and fixed acquisitions with matched budgets and held-out prediction improvement.

Restrictions of this milestone
No transformer has been trained, no real material has been inferred, no new
benchmark superiority is claimed. Friction and arbitrary editable rigid geometries
are not supported by this new wrapper yet. Coarse FEM is not a physical reference.
The existing broader v18 backend remains available for multi-object extensions.

Completed first-pilot evidence
Seven unit tests and eleven artifact/impulse/video checks pass. All ten controlled
rollouts finished with finite states and positive sampled element Jacobians.
Maximum lift/release non-rigid deformation was13.31mm. The12kPa drop timestep
halving changed positions by mean0.0114mm and maximum0.1000mm. Maximum sampled
floor penetration across main pilots was1.013mm. These are coarse numerical
checks, not reference-simulator qualification or real-world physical accuracy.
Prescribed-force impulse errors are below1e-7Ns (observed near3e-16Ns).
Matching author tracks/calibration can be acquired with:
runs/phystwin-env/bin/python -m scripts.editable_scene_v23.acquire_labels
Raw RGB download may still be running; acquisition-snapshot.json is timestamped,
while runs/editable-scene-v23/acquisition-status.json is the live local status.
