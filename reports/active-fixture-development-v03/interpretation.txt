DEVELOPMENT RESULTS — active-policy advantage remains untested

The task uses a three-dimensional block with both ends clamped and one extra compliant fixture at an unknown one of four off-center bottom locations. Material stiffness and support stiffness are also unknown. Eight oblique pokes have equal integrated force budgets. Four initial measurements are partial; two different held loads test later prediction.

The completed linear screen retains all 72 planned mesh/parameter/layout states and 792 load responses. At mesh 6, thirty of 54 support pairs have initial four-channel differences with whitened length no greater than 1. Eight of these thirty pairs differ by more than 20 micrometers RMS under held loads; their held-load differences reach 60.075 micrometers. This is a deterministic development-grid separation diagnostic, not population statistics, posterior ambiguity or a policy result.

The original narrow-poke nonlinear pilot failed strain and mesh checks; those failures remain recorded. A broader Gaussian load preserves geometry, support priors and force directions. Refining the SAME Gaussian experiment from meshes 3 to 6 to 12 passes all six physical/excitation controls and both spatial gates. The fine poke produces 0.391 mm coordinate nonrigid RMS, roughly half total motion. Its public-point mesh difference is 9.067 micrometers, below the unchanged 20 micrometer limit. Maximum sampled strain is 4.866 percent, below 5 percent. These six controls do not validate every layout, parameter or load; sampled Jacobians and numerical stability diagnostics are not continuum certificates.

The first finite parameter grid changed the preferred action when refined. A same-observation continuous-reference repair now qualifies all four layout categories and all eight action utilities. All 48 action/bank/outcome refinement jobs pass, with a resolved numerical ranking for that saved earlier linear development case. Refinement differences are indicators, not rigorous error bounds. All three GPUs reproduce the CPU reference; the finer one-action GPU utility takes 1.47 seconds. Raw observations, fields and posterior/risk arrays remain local.

The fixed 36-case Gaussian development screen is now complete and independently audited. All 144 continuous category references, 180 decision banks and 1,728 utility jobs qualified numerically. All 36 cases have resolved action rankings; six different actions win somewhere. The unchanged scenario-adequacy criteria nevertheless fail: the second most common winner occurs on seven objects, below the required eight, and meaningful regret from the strongest universal fixed action occurs on eight objects, below the required nine. All objects remain in the denominator. These negative results do not justify full nonlinear expansion or an active-probing advantage claim.

A separate diagnostic of the previously completed linear mechanics screen found the downward prediction load contributes 89.4 percent of support-sensitive target energy. Among the thirty especially similar initial-observation support pairs it contributes 95.0 percent. These are deterministic signal diagnostics, not posterior-risk decompositions. The mechanics concept was selected from the closed linear screen before reading the new 36-case policy outcomes.

The next version changes only the two future prediction loads to rotationally related oblique loads, each with the same 0.06 N force magnitude as before. This is a new development prediction task, not an improvement on the old task. The same objects, saved initial observations, eight poke choices, priors, query positions and adequacy thresholds remain fixed. Equal force magnitudes do not guarantee balanced responses or a useful action policy; the new task must be checked. Source and independent review are in progress. No new actual experiment, nonlinear expansion or fresh held-test draw has begun.
