Completed simulation campaign: position-conditioned deformation prediction

The model receives three partial position observations and prescribed tool poses,
not true forces or material labels. It infers material variation, compliant tool
coupling, gravity direction and a spatial interaction field, then predicts settled
shape under new tool translations and rotations. A further positional observation
can refine the explanation and its predictive uncertainty.

Completed evaluation
42 independent primary test objects: 18 familiar parameters, 12 material shifts,
12 environmental shifts. Physical-component comparisons use the same predeclared
18-object subset. Nine familiar objects are reused for translation/probing; six
additional objects test two unseen environmental force laws. All 394 primary job
outputs, 36 force-law predictions and the complete acquisition study are present.
All 93 reference fields pass their declared numerical qualification criteria.

On nine transfer objects, one extra response reduces held-target error from
0.314 to 0.084 mm (73.2%). The sensitivity policy selects the same action as the
strong fixed policy on every object; random probing gives 0.163 mm. Mean interval
coverage improves from 81.9% to 92.0% while mean half-width falls from 0.373 to
0.157 mm. Six of nine objects reach 90% coordinate coverage after probing.
Receiving the same response, material-indexed loading gives 0.297 mm versus
0.084 mm for the spatial model. Before probing, neither frame is clearly better.

Across the six unseen-force-law objects, new-motion errors are 0.075 mm for ours,
0.288 mm for MPM and 0.798 mm for PhysTwin; translation errors are 0.330, 0.546
and 0.859 mm, respectively. Individual law groups contain only three objects.

Interpretation and scope
The evidence supports position-driven prediction and useful one-response
adaptation when material and environment are unknown. It does not establish an
adaptive-policy advantage over the strong fixed probe, unique material recovery,
or a general advantage over the complete published visual pipelines. Physical
baselines are documented adaptations of official simulation components. The
reference, our model and the neural training targets share a Neo-Hookean family;
material OOD changes its coefficients and heterogeneity. Geometry, correspondence
and density are known. Videos show actual-scale settled queries with camera
orbits, not dynamic forecasts, impacts or drops. All optimizer termination messages
and fixed-budget outcomes are retained in the compute report and supplement.
