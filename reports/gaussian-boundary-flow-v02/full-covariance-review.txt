FULL-COVARIANCE MIXTURE ABLATION — FROZEN CONFIRMATION RESULTS
4 November 2026

Predeclared question
Does modeling correlated parameter uncertainty improve the deficient support-
location coverage of the diagonal mixture? A two-component full-covariance model
(141516 parameters) was compared with the existing four-component diagonal mixture
(141713), flow(141894), and deterministic regressor(140996).

Training and validation
Same clean8192-case bank, one50um minibatch-noise draw, same minibatch/noise stream,
AdamW3e-4 and15000 steps/batch256. New validation seed63119 (64 cases) was used only
for finite likelihood/factors/samples and finite physical rollouts, with no tuning.
The final checkpoint and validation result were sealed before generating the new
128-case confirmation cohort, seed13401. All four frozen models received the same
context; all probabilistic methods propagated32 samples. GPU2 was used for physics;
CPU training took63 seconds and physical validation/confirmation took97 seconds.

Result: no evidence supporting replacement of the diagonal predictive candidate.
Program        Flow      Diagonal mixture   Full covariance    Point regressor
Sine           .205 mm      .162 mm             .197 mm             .360 mm
Release        .197 mm      .133 mm             .165 mm             .336 mm
Triangle       .204 mm      .156 mm             .189 mm             .357 mm
Reversal       .214 mm      .199 mm             .237 mm             .344 mm

The diagonal mixture has lower mean prediction error than full covariance on all
four programs. Paired95% bootstrap intervals include zero, so this is a consistent
mean ranking, not a conclusive statistical superiority claim on this sample.
Diagonal predictive interval scores also beat full covariance on every program.

Primary support-location check
Coverage rises78.9% ->85.2%, but the paired coverage-difference95% interval is
[-2.34,+14.84] percentage points, so improvement is inconclusive. Full covariance
widens support-location intervals1.09 ->1.76mm and increases mean absolute error
.265 ->.420mm. The support-location interval score worsens1.45 ->2.38mm. Therefore
we should not describe the higher coverage alone as better calibrated inference.
Both models remain below90% support-location coverage.

Other uncertainty measures
- Full covariance improves logit NLL: -4.028 versus diagonal -3.644 (lower better).
- Its normalized multivariate parameter energy score worsens: .0994 versus .0863.
- Flow has the best parameter energy score here, .0538, despite weaker mean-motion
  predictions. Different objectives should remain visible rather than selecting
  the metric favoring a preferred model.
- Diagonal predictive coordinate coverage is90.9-93.0%; full covariance86.8-90.0%.
- Pointwise32-sample intervals are not simultaneous trajectory guarantees.

Interpretation and next decision
The conditional reference justified testing correlation, but correlation flexibility
alone did not solve the observed parameter-coverage weakness. Retain the diagonal
mixture as the economical predictive candidate, and keep flow for comparison on
joint physical uncertainty. Do not add still more covariance/model complexity
before resolving the real-data forward-model and prior-domain mismatch.

Limits of this ablation
One training seed per architecture; no optimization on the confirmation set.
Component count changed from four diagonal to two full-covariance components to
match parameter counts, so this is not an isolated covariance-only causal test.
All data still share the known block geometry, mass, initial state and supplied
boundary topology. No real-data or generalized-contact claim follows.
CPU/GPU maximum displacement difference is3.64um on four fresh cases; comparisons
use the same float32 GPU solver and should not be interpreted as exact physics.
