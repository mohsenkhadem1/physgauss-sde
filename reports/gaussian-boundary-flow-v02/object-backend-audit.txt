INDEPENDENT AUDIT: geometry-specific object boundary backend
Auditor /root/flow_numerical_audit; 2026-11-04.
Reviewed object_backend.py, object_campaign.py, benchmark_inputs.py and the reused
ExternalFEM. Original running backend/campaign were not modified.

Data separation
No direct suffix object-motion access was found in the inverse campaign. The
predictor loader exports prefix object tracks/masks plus supplied initial geometry
and complete prescribed controller schedules. The mask uses incoming transitions.
Future controllers are legitimate conditional action inputs, not unknown future
object targets. Upstream processed tracks/initial geometry are not certified
online-causal; the existing disclosed limitation remains necessary. This is a
historically exposed sequence and geometry-specific simulator bank, not zero-shot
real-video generalization.

Parameter/unit review
Theta index mapping is correct: gravity ratio, per-grip attachment ratios, floor
ratio; footprint exponents follow separately and material heterogeneity is zero.
Renormalizing powered Gaussian weights separates footprint from total attachment
stiffness. No absolute material/force scale is identified. Current shape is a
convex-hull mesh, including fictitious tissue between limbs; this remains a pilot
rather than a faithful plush-object mechanical discretization.

BLOCKER 1: floor hypothesis materially controls motion
ExternalFEM applies its normal floor penalty even when contact=False; that flag
only disables the optional tangential compliance. The guessed floor is at the
initial lowest point and the inferred coefficient cannot turn off under the
original log range [3,8]. Four exposed sloth prior worlds (neutral + seed-913
three draws; 64 nodes, prefix only) were compared with exact coefficient zero:
World   Relative-to-first motion difference, floor on vs off (dense continuation)
0       26.289968 mm coordinate RMS
1        2.820717 mm
2       32.849544 mm
3       11.124689 mm
All solves converged. These differences are much larger than numerical residuals
or sub-mm uncertainty claims. Treat floor presence as an explicit hypothesis and
compare it using development observations; do not infer a true bottom support
from successful optimization of a forced floor model.

Schedule check: no observed blocker in these four worlds
Original sparse12 versus dense-prefix solve schedules differ by only
0.000004-0.000112 mm RMS. Thus the potential nonlinear branch issue is not observed
here. It remains a contract risk for other shapes/commands, because warm-start
continuation is history dependent. The new optional subclass uses a canonical
all-command-frame path; output sampling alone cannot alter its solve path.

BLOCKER 2: incremental rebasing is not the mechanical state
The campaign exports X0 + x(t) - x(0), where x(0) is its first solved equilibrium.
This is a potentially nonuniform nodal offset, not a rigid coordinate change.
Internal minJ/residual checks certify x(t), not the rebased mesh. Independent
checks on the same four worlds give:
                       Floor on                 Floor off
Initial offset RMS     0.052-2.518 mm            3.491-156.854 mm
Internal max residual  <9.8e-8                   <9.8e-8
Rebased max residual   3.41e-4 to 1.68e-2        9.60e-4 to 2.29e-2
No rebased element inverted in these eight runs. Residuals are dimensionless and
should not be relabeled as force errors in N. Large no-floor initial mismatches
show that a physically admissible solver state is not necessarily consistent
with the supplied initial shape/attachment observation.

Recommendation: retain raw mechanical trajectories as the physical output, and
report their initial-shape mismatch. Incremental rebasing may remain a separately
named observation-space tracking map, but must not be presented as a force-balanced
renderable physical state. Initial prestrain/rest-shape uncertainty needs explicit
modeling before absolute physical interpretation. Do not add fixed dead loads to
cancel the initial residual: they would persist after release and create fictional
support. None was implemented by this audit.

Uncertainty reporting
- Node quantiles cannot be pushed through observation interpolation. Project each
  accepted trajectory with A first and THEN take observation-space quantiles. A
  new test gives a simple counterexample where mapping node 95% quantiles yields
  zero while the true interpolated 95% quantile is .5.
- Retain accepted posterior samples/indices, not just marginal lo/hi arrays.
- Failed posterior rollouts condition the distribution on numerical feasibility;
  keep failure rates and avoid treating discarded draws as no uncertainty cost.
- Sixteen draws give coarse tail estimates; this is predictive spread, not proven
  90% coverage. Calibrate using an independent synthetic/reference discrepancy
  study rather than declaring real coverage from this one exposed object.
- Clarify whether 1 mm noise is defined on relative coordinates conditional on
  the initial observation. If original observations have independent noise, their
  shared first-frame subtraction induces correlated noise across later frames.
