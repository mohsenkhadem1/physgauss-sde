Bilateral versus unilateral Gaussian boundary audit and bounded test proposal

Current mechanism: ExternalFEM.evaluate uses E_attach = 1/2 sum_i a(t) k w_i ||x_i-y_i(t)||^2. The Gaussian w_i is initialized from controller proximity in material coordinates; the inferred footprint changes its width, not its physical contact state. The PhysTwin adapter sets a(t)=1 at all times. Therefore moving a pushing hand away pulls the same material patch after geometric separation, and tangential motion is always tethered. This is a valid compliant adhesive/grasp hypothesis, not a general push-contact law. Changing posterior density, Gaussian width, material heterogeneity or table friction does not remove that mismatch. Actual contact labels/normals are not measured in these packages; do not claim the cause of a particular forecast failure has been isolated without ablation.

Proposed minimal alternative, no implementation in this audit:
For a prescribed local tool plane (q(t), n(t)), define signed gap
    g_i(t) = n(t)^T [x_i - q(t)],
with g>0 the noncontact side and n pointing from tool into allowed object space. Use a fixed nonnegative material patch w_i and positive inferred stiffness k:
    E_push = 1/2 k sum_i w_i [min(g_i,0)]^2.
Then the force is f_i = k w_i max(-g_i,0) n: compressive only, exactly zero at separation, no arbitrary tangential tether. Its active-region tangent is k w_i n n^T. This quadratic penalty is C1 at contact and piecewise C2; use the exact active set or explicitly disclose any smoothing. It is a soft nonpenetration penalty, not hard contact, friction or impact physics. Keep the Gaussian patch fixed in this first test so the potential derivative is unambiguous. A world-space moving Gaussian gate later requires differentiating the gate too; simply multiplying force by a changing gate need not preserve a stated potential.

Tool normal and patch are not free future-fit quantities. Freeze a tool-frame plane from initial controller geometry when it is geometrically identifiable. If only point trajectories are available, a prefix-only normal hypothesis may be initialized from tool approach direction with explicit sign/offset ambiguity; select among predeclared hypotheses using permitted subprefix observations only, or report unsupported orientation. Do not infer normal from future object motion. Tool pose transforms the plane with proper rigid rotations. A local plane is only a local contact approximation; finite support and curved geometry remain limitations.

Test as a scenario-specific boundary ablation, not a universal replacement. Retain bilateral attachments for known held/lift examples. On the exposed pushing example compare the original bilateral law against this zero-tension unilateral law at matched geometry, prefix observations, forward-solver budget and parameter count. Keep body gravity/table terms unchanged and do not simultaneously change material capacity. Prefix-only adequacy oracle first: does the new family reduce discarded-space mismatch and avoid false post-separation pulling? Freeze any subsequent posterior bank before suffix scoring. Report the entire prefix and forecast, not just favorable frames.

Required synthetic unit/qualification checks before real comparison: force exactly zero for positive gap; compressive force sign for negative gap; potential-gradient/Hessian consistency away from transition; prescribed tool withdrawal releases instead of pulling; covariance under joint rotation/translation of object and tool; static penetration decreases with stiffness without solver degeneration. Check lift regressions separately with the original bilateral branch. No impact/restitution, calibrated contact probability or general-contact claim follows from passing these checks.

An optional future latent contact-mode mixture would distinguish adhesive/bilateral from unilateral hypotheses, but video ambiguity can make both consistent with a prefix. That is a model-discrepancy/identifiability question, not a reason to make current uncertainty intervals narrower. The next justified experiment is the one-operator matched ablation above, after the frozen eight-case expansion, not a large contact architecture.
