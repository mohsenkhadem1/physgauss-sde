Structural differences verified in source
1. v02 has zero material coefficients: bind() allocates theta=zeros and writes gravity, attachment, floor and width parameters only. Therefore materials=1 is genuinely uniform here, not a learned heterogeneous material field. It fixes Poisson ratio to 0.35. Archived v22 analytic FEM fits SIX spatial Gaussian material coefficients, and its neural variant adds a frozen learned constitutive decoder plus three global response parameters. v22 also uses 240 nodes and 18 fitted frames versus this pilot's 64 and 12. These differences are confounded; unmatched archived scores cannot establish which caused failure.
Sources: object_backend_wide.py::bind; external_benchmark/fem.py::evaluate; real_transfer_v19/neural_fem.py; real_transfer_v22/campaign.py.

2. v02 is quasistatic continuation from initial-rest geometry, not an evolving physical state initialized at the observed prefix end. Every requested output follows successive equilibrium solves; time/velocity do not enter. Gaussian material attachment centers are initialized once and only their width/strength vary. PhysTwin adapter sets attachment_active=ones, so a pushing or separating tool remains a bilateral spring indefinitely. Neither a narrow nor wide Gaussian gives unilateral contact, release history or momentum. Wider material flexibility alone cannot repair these structural boundary/state errors.
Sources: object_backend_revision.py::rollout; benchmark_inputs.py::load_phystwin_predictor; external_benchmark/fem.py::evaluate.

3. Archived SoftVT v14 is a different predictive system: it preserves the original v12 baseline, estimates the terminal observed object pose in the controller frame, fits an eight-frame gravity-aligned relative slip velocity, and triggers a reduced release/contact pose rollout after a geometric overlap budget. Its split integrator retains shape at separation. v02's optional tracking output only subtracts simulated FRAME ZERO; it does not assimilate terminal pose or carry prefix velocity. Therefore v14's principal transport/release machinery has been removed, not superseded by the new posterior.
Sources: benchmark_improvement_v14/softvt_predict.py; softvt_slip.py; contact_benchmark_v12/object_frame.py; contact_rollout.py; package_campaign.py.

One next backend change recommended
Restore a PREFIX-INITIALIZED RIGID POSE STATE around the present FEM deformation, as an isolated nested backend, before expanding density capacity or material dimensions. At the permitted prefix endpoint, use the same sparse points/mask to estimate a proper rigid pose and a short-window linear/angular velocity. Express deformation in that moving object frame. During held intervals transport pose through the known controller motion with the prefix object/controller offset; at known release continue a separate pose state using the archived explicit release rule. Keep the unmodified v03 backend as the zero-correction control. This directly restores the large missing v14 capability and targets the observed pose-dominated SoftVT gap. It should not be advertised as fully coupled contact: impulses do not yet alter FEM shape, release/impact qualification remains separate, and nonzero angular transport must be tested for objectivity.

Bounded decision test: use only the already exposed two pilots, identical v03 banks, posterior samples, prefix budgets and suffix scoring. Do not retrain density. Add paired raw, rigid-pose-only and current tracking outputs; require prefix-end continuity, no suffix inputs, unchanged aligned shape under a pure rigid correction, all solver failures preserved, and forecast coordinate improvement without hiding shape error. This test can fail, particularly on push sloth, whose large shape error requires later boundary/contact and/or material/mesh diagnosis. Before adding six material modes, run a matched prefix-only oracle to determine whether that addition actually reduces discarded-space mismatch. Do not claim the pose correction identifies stiffness/contact or solves the push-contact problem.

Why this change first: it is nested, requires no unqualified impact training bank, and addresses an explicitly removed successful mechanism and the dominant measured SoftVT error. A richer material field may help plush shape but cannot create inertia, detachment or terminal-state assimilation. A larger posterior cannot represent trajectories missing from its forward family.
