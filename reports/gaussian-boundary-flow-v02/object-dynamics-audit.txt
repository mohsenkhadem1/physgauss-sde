Independent dynamic integration audit

Original backward Euler implementation correctly uses physical time, inertia to
remove rigid nullspaces, raw initial mesh, and no output rebasing. Its fixed mass
is consistent with the gravity/material ratio: acceleration in metres is 9.81.
The submitted drop-qualification.json does NOT qualify impact: bounce height
changes 9.07 -> 17.62 -> 27.15 mm under dt halving, despite tiny solve residuals.
That is discretization error, not uncertainty about material. A learned posterior
must never absorb this damping by fitting spring/density/floor parameters.

Additional original-backend issues:
* Activation at a command boundary is applied throughout the preceding final
  substep, an O(dt) early switch. Midpoint below uses the left interval activation.
* Checking residual > tolerance alone does not reject NaN. Isolated midpoint
  rejects nonfinite residuals and checks endpoint J as well as midpoint J.
* Original drop-report minJ only samples retained outputs; qualification must examine
  every step. Isolated midpoint keeps full-path minimum J and penetration.
* A compliant normal plane is not hard nonpenetration or frictional impact.
* Newton convergence cannot replace time-resolution qualification.

Isolated alternative: object_midpoint.py
No edits to root BE implementation. For step h, solve at y:
  grad V(y,t+h/2) + (4M/h^2)(y-x-h*v/2) = 0.
This reuses DynamicFEM energy minimization with inertial_dt=h/2. Export
  x_new = 2y-x; v_new = 2(x_new-x)/h-v.
This is implicit midpoint; it preserves quadratic oscillator energy and is
second order for smooth forces. Unilateral contact onset is nonsmooth and still
requires refinement. No velocity projections, rebasing, hidden restitution, or
state corrections are applied. We reject an inverted extrapolated endpoint.

Tests: 3 midpoint tests + 3 unchanged BE tests pass. Midpoint tests establish exact
freefall (including initial velocity) and conserved total energy, requested-output
sampling invariance, and second-order smooth spring accuracy against an analytic
oscillator. That oscillator also conserves kinetic+spring+gravity energy.

The first coarse midpoint ladder is preserved as midpoint-coarse-qualification.json.
It is unqualified: dt=.005 rebound138.75mm; .0025 failed line search near 3e-8
residual; .00125 rebound88.81; .000625 rebound47.28; .0003125 rebound46.61;
.00015625 rebound47.24. Last trajectory changes are 2.01 and1.37mm, exceeding
0.1mm. No numerical gate was relaxed after these results.

Fine ladder in midpoint-qualification.json additionally evaluates physical bulk,
attachment, gravity and floor potential plus kinetic energy on every substep.
Static gauge and inertial penalty are excluded exactly by evaluating DynamicFEM
with its inertial target at the current state. No driving/damping exists in the
qualified drop. Energy errors are normalized by initial gravitational potential
above the physical plane, not the arbitrary zero of potential energy. Gates are
trajectory change <=0.1mm, rebound change <=1%, energy error <=1% of initial drop
potential, on two successive refinements. Fine result is reported in that JSON;
absence of admission means impact must stay excluded from benchmark claims.

Final bounded decision: NOT ADMITTED. Fine ladder64/128/256substeps completed
onCPU withoutGPU. At64substeps, maximum energy error0.23877%, final loss0.07349%,
maximum penetration1.55414mm. The128and256 refinements failed the unchanged
1e-7 solve-residual acceptance gate. This may involve cancellation from increasingly
stiff inertial terms, but the specific cause is not established. No solver tolerance
or convergence threshold was loosened. Preserve this evidence, exclude impact
claims from training/benchmarks, and return to informative smooth/quasistatic cases.
Improving nonlinear solve residual scaling could be a future bounded task; neither
