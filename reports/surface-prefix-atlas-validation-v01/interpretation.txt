The previous study found that the inference numerics could converge while the material intervals became too confident under nonlinear simulation data. This phase addresses the forward-model mismatch before testing calibration again.

The new response model adds a smooth nonlinear correction to the exact linear finite-element response. For the initial force, it uses 81 fixed parameter-grid simulations and 32 reserved validation simulations. The 9-by-9 correction passed all predeclared response, refinement and observation-likelihood checks. Its largest reserved-point displacement error was 6.18e-12 metres; the 5-by-5 versus 9-by-9 difference was 7.41e-8 metres. These compare interpolation against the same finite-element equations. They do not measure the simulator's error relative to reality or remove mesh error.

Both process-monitor failures are retained. The first interrupted aggregation after 108 successful simulations; the explicit recovery completed the remaining five successfully before a second exit-monitor race. A separate validation-only step reused all 113 verified archives without running another simulation. An independent reviewer reproduced its validation results exactly.

The nonlinear posterior implementation has passed synthetic integration controls, but has not yet established calibrated uncertainty on physical observations. Next steps are to validate all ten forces, qualify an actual nonlinear posterior against independent numerical integration, then test linear and nonlinear inference on a fresh 64-object cohort. The original 16 objects remain development data. No improved calibration or active-probing advantage is claimed here.
