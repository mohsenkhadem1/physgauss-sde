The controlled 3D probing scenario now passes its prospective development-adequacy criteria. The block has both ends clamped, an extra compliant bottom fixture at one of four unknown locations, unknown material and fixture stiffness, four initial measurements and eight equal-force oblique poke choices.

The earlier downward/axial future-load task remains a recorded negative: its second common winning action occurred on seven objects versus eight required, and meaningful strongest-fixed-action regret on eight versus nine required. No cases or thresholds were changed to repair that result.

The new development task changes only the two future prediction loads to opposing sideways/downward directions at rotationally related locations. Objects, saved initial observations, candidate pokes, query points, material/support priors and adequacy thresholds are unchanged. Across all 54 same-material support pairs, the two new loads contribute 50.04% and 49.96% of support-sensitive squared displacement, compared with 89.40% and 10.60% for the earlier pair. These are deterministic signal contributions, not posterior-risk decompositions.

All 36 objects passed the numerical checks: 144 continuous category references, 180 decision banks and 1,728 utility jobs. Independent audit reproduced the rankings and adequacy arithmetic. Two actions are the unique winners on 16 and 13 objects, respectively; 35 objects have a unique winner and one remains tied. 35/36 satisfy the ambiguity/between-layout criteria. On 11/36, choosing the best action instead of the strongest universal fixed action reduces predicted risk by at least 5% of prior target variance after subtracting numerical refinement indicators. Those indicators are not rigorous error bounds. Every case, including the tie, remains in the denominator.

The 72 new held-load linear equilibria pass original-residual, exact-clamp, known-load balance and modal/direct checks. Maximum modal/direct difference is 9.875e-16 m. Initial observations are reused byte-for-byte, and unchanged prefix evidence/support probabilities are checked against the previous run. Wall time was 1,291.53 s and peak aggregate memory 6.44 GiB using CPUs and GPUs 0–2; GPU 3 was untouched. Raw fields, observations, posterior arrays and media remain local.

The new six-field nonlinear pilot is now complete and independently audited. Both new held loads were solved for the same fixed soft object (E = 2,000 Pa, fixture stiffness = 5 N/m, first support layout) at meshes 3, 6 and 12. All six pass the unchanged physics, excitation and eligibility criteria; both pass the unchanged spatial checks. Nonrigid coordinate RMS is 540–557 micrometers, roughly 53–54% of total motion. Maximum sampled strain is 4.565%, below 5%, and minimum sampled J is 0.971. Finest-mesh query differences are 9.96 and 10.34 micrometers, below 20. Conditional fine indicators are 1.32 and 1.44 micrometers; these are numerical refinement indicators, not rigorous continuum bounds. The run took 1,306 seconds with peak aggregate memory about 13.28 GiB. All owned processes exited cleanly.

The local eight-second video shows both prescribed static states with a moving camera and displacement magnified 20 times. It compares the nonlinear reference with the linear oracle using the SAME true material and support values. At 16 public markers, their coordinate RMS differences are 26.18 and 24.93 micrometers. This isolates forward-model approximation error for this controlled object; it is not inference error or a learned forecasting result. Both decoded preset frames were visually reviewed and all 96 frames decoded successfully. Media and raw arrays remain local.

The useful result is that support uncertainty and poke choice now matter in a mechanically checked 3D task. Earlier load/mesh failures and the old scenario-adequacy negative remain published and unchanged. The two positive studies have distinct scopes: 36 noiseless matching-linear development objects for decision adequacy, and one fixed material/support setting for nonlinear three-mesh controls. Neither establishes calibrated generalized boundary learning, observed noisy probing advantage, continuous nonlinear posterior convergence across all supports, or held-out generalization. No fresh test draws were used and the full nonlinear bank remains unadmitted.

The next comparison must measure realized held-load prediction error after a noisy poke against the strongest fixed and uniform-random policies, and separately test nonlinear model mismatch. Weak stiff-object responses must remain in the denominator with the original excitation flags reported. Any new separation of reference validity from excitation goals requires an explicit prospective protocol; none was used to relabel these completed results.
