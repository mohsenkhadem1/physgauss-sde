PIDG: solver-driven future-frame qualification
============================================

OUTCOME: target not met. Solver full-image PSNR, LPIPS and flow agreement are worse
than native PIDG on all three scenes. Balls foreground PSNR improves, but remains
below constant velocity. The binding already loses substantial observed-prefix quality.

Training: original fitting frames through 60% of sequence time.
Model selection: forecast-validation frames at 60-70%.
Final evaluation: released test frames after 70%; 11/15/15 images.
These public datasets were previously exposed; this is a new chronological protocol.
Old full-sequence appearance checkpoints are excluded. Previous paper results are unchanged.

INTERPRETATION AND LIMITS
All methods use prefix-only appearance initialization and the same fitting observations.
Native PIDG chooses base/refined on validation. Hybrid has the previously fixed 1e-5 weight.
Solver adds static appearance fitting and RGB multiple-shooting physical identification.
A constant-velocity control shares the bound appearance and selects velocity filtering on validation.
Balls has six first-fitting-frame-assisted instances; the others use occupancy components.
Volumes and units are inferred proxies. No true 3D states, masses, materials or forces are supplied.
Gaussian fields represent unknown interactions; this adapter does not enforce explicit contact.
Centroid/flow are image-motion proxies. Positive Jacobians and conservative internal forces
are numerical sanity checks, not a certificate of real physical accuracy.
Temporal-field Jacobians and FEM Jacobians use different representations; do not turn them
into an unqualified physical-error leaderboard.
The first raw-velocity duck attempt failed; it is retained. Target-free diagnostics showed
that decreasing dt did not cure the problem; filtering nonrigid initial velocities did.
Changes were driven by fitting/validation and stability diagnostics, not final-test scores.
Final figures use the middle declared test frame and shared reference crops; videos show
all sparse recorded test frames. No deformation exaggeration or per-method alignment.
Raw datasets/checkpoints/trajectories stay local. No novel-view or new-force ground truth is invented.
