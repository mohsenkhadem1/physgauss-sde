COUPLED CONTACT V13: PHYSICS AUDIT
Status: frozen-source audit complete; final case results and video inspection pending.

Implemented mechanics
The deformable block uses conforming linear tetrahedra and compressible Neo-Hookean elastic energy. Stress is its analytic derivative; objective viscous stress depends on the symmetric spatial velocity gradient. This is nonlinear FEM, not the older spring-lattice qualification. Gravity, prescribed collider poses/velocities, frictional contact, and movable spherical rigid bodies are explicit. The native comparison changes internal elasticity to its imported spring kernel while sharing contact and integration.

Contact is unilateral penalty contact at boundary vertices. Rest boundary-triangle area is lumped to vertices and scales stiffness relative to a fixed 0.0004 m^2 patch; interior vertices do not collide. This reduces arbitrary resolution dependence but does not establish mesh convergence. Tangential force is Coulomb-capped viscous friction with regularization speed 0.01 m/s. There is no exact static-friction constraint or tangential displacement history. A 'stick' phase is approximate, not a complementarity solution.

Soft-to-rigid forces and torques are equal and opposite. Sphere–static-shape and sphere–sphere interactions use a shared point for the two reaction torques. Dynamic rigid contact is a separate point-penalty model with unscaled collider stiffness. Only spheres can move dynamically; prescribed tools and fixed obstacles do not have inferred mass/inertia. The sphere's geometry is rotation invariant; its spin enters contact velocity, but orientation is not tracked. The current scenarios do not establish coupled dynamics for arbitrary rigid objects or two deformable objects.

Known geometric/integration limits
Contact samples vertices, not surface triangles, and does not provide continuous collision detection, self-contact, or a global nonintersection guarantee. Positive tet volume is a local feasibility test, not collision-free topology. Explicit symplectic steps reject J <= 0.02 and nonfinite states; no failed state is silently repaired. Output-time topology inspection cannot alone detect an intermediate collision. Physics diagnostics record minimum J at every integration step. The open hemispherical bowl implementation exists but is not exercised by the frozen curved-obstacle scenario, which uses a fixed convex sphere. The floor and edge are analytic geometry.

Development decision audit
The archived initial qualification retains a failed curved geometry: fine-mesh penetration exceeded the 1 mm threshold and timestep discrepancy exceeded 0.25 mm. The obstacle was moved laterally before method freeze; the final center is (0.085, 0.055, 0.033) m. The original failed record remains separate. Other scenarios were unchanged and reuse their explicitly recorded earlier hashes.
Final development qualification contains ten scenario/mesh comparisons (five scenarios, two resolutions, three timesteps each) and five softer-material finest-only stress probes. Every declared gate passes. At the finer mesh, 0.05 vs 0.025 ms full-trajectory coordinate RMS errors are approximately: squeeze 0.00064 mm; slide 0.00342 mm; moving sphere 0.00184 mm; curved obstacle 0.00197 mm; edge drop 0.01723 mm. The soft stress probes are stability/contact checks, not independent convergence studies. Those E=3000 Pa, friction=0.1 probes have maximum penetration 0.561 mm and minimum J 0.349. Their maximum rigid-aligned deformation reaches 10.32 mm in edge drop. The 13 physics unit tests cover force/energy consistency, objectivity, momentum/reaction balance, friction bounds, geometric queries, area weighting and inversion rejection.

Inference and claim boundaries
Calibration uses sparse noisy positions from squeeze/shear, not measured forces. Tool positions, initial geometry and scene geometry are known. Density, Poisson ratio and viscosity are fixed; only a discrete stiffness/regularized-friction grid is selected. Native spring coefficient is not a recovered Young modulus. Saturated friction counts show whether the experiment reaches the Coulomb-capped regime, but do not establish unique friction identification. Calibration score gaps and grid-edge selections are diagnostics, not calibrated uncertainty intervals. Coarse/fine FEM uses the same constitutive family, so this is controlled discretization transfer, not independent simulator or real-world validation. A low error cannot by itself prove recovered true parameters because coarse-mesh and contact errors can be absorbed into fitted parameters.

The frozen campaign tests unseen interaction types relative to squeeze calibration. Test material values lie within a prescribed range, not outside all training material ranges. It does not re-run SoftVTBench/PhysTwin or demonstrate new improvements on those external datasets. Existing external results should remain separately preserved. Physics improvements demonstrated here should be described as qualified coupled-contact capability and controlled cross-interaction prediction, contingent on final per-case qualification.

Final numerical results, gate failures, selections and fixed video review will be appended after outputs exist. No frozen source changes are authorized by this audit.

POST-FREEZE DEVELOPMENT SPATIAL AUDIT
Mesh diagnosis evaluates common physical rest points by the actual tetrahedral P1 basis. It reveals substantial spatial/contact sensitivity despite passed temporal gates. With known parameters, cells4 versus6 full-trajectory errors are edge dev0 12.13 mm and dev1 29.72 mm; final orientation differences are57.15 and105.64 degrees. Curved4 versus6 differences are0.872 and2.497 mm. Edge references are therefore not established spatially converged. Odd/even node placement changes initial edge-top sampling; n3 has no initial vertex column over the16 mm edge, though later side/deformation contacts do occur. This can change tipping branches. The frozen benchmark must retain these cases and this limitation. A separately declared surface-quadrature extension is now being developed; it must not replace historical source or silently relabel historical outcomes.

FROZEN ORIGINAL DEVELOPMENT INFERENCE CHECK
FEM selects (E=6500 Pa, mu=0.6) for dev0 whose generating parameters are (6000 Pa, 0.35), and (3000 Pa, 0.2) for dev1 generated at (3000 Pa, 0.1). The observed calibration coordinate RMSE is 0.495 and 0.961 mm, respectively, versus observation-noise standard deviation 0.1 mm. Thus model/discretization discrepancy remains materially larger than measurement noise. Profiling stiffness out of the fixed mu grid [0,0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8] gives dev0 calibration RMSE [2.838,2.429,0.552,0.495,0.710] mm and dev1 [1.677,0.961,2.248,2.587,2.489] mm. These profiles show sensitivity to friction and a chosen optimum, but do not demonstrate unbiased recovery or calibrated uncertainty. In particular dev0's favored friction differs from the generating value, consistent with compensating coarse-model error. No force observations enter selection.
