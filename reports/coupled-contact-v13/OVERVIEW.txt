COUPLED CONTACT v13: COMPLETED ORIGINAL STUDY, REFERENCE REPAIR ONGOING

What we built
A soft tetrahedral block now changes shape while exchanging forces with rigid
tools, a floor, an edge, a curved obstacle and a freely moving sphere. Contact
can move both bodies. The model observes noisy positions during one squeeze,
sideways shear and release, chooses stiffness and friction from a fixed grid,
then predicts four different interactions from their initial states and tool
trajectories. It is not given future object motion or true material labels.

What the original frozen experiment found
All 56 forecast cases were retained: 8 development, 16 validation and 32 reserved.
On the 32 reserved forecasts (8 material/friction combinations x 4 interactions):
  Inferred FEM                 13.588 mm world-position RMSE; 32/32 complete
  Fixed nominal FEM            15.729 mm; 32/32 complete
  Rigid body + same contact    20.081 mm; 32/32 complete
  True-parameter coarse FEM    12.047 mm; 32/32 complete
  Adapted native spring core   33.374 mm; 20/32 complete, conditional mean
  Position ridge               49.630 mm; 32/32 complete
The inferred model beats rigid motion in 28/32 cases and nominal FEM in 25/32.
Its mean improvement is 32.3% over rigid and 13.6% over nominal. Shape-only error
improves more modestly:1.782 versus 1.915 mm for rigid. On 20 jointly completed
native comparisons, inferred FEM averages 15.612 mm and wins 19/20. Native failures
hit the shared positive-tetrahedron-volume guard. This is an adaptation using
the authors' unchanged PhysTwin spring-force core with our contact/integrator,
not the complete authors' method or an official PhysTwin benchmark ranking.

Why this is not yet physical validation
The reference passed timestep/penetration checks on 26/32 reserved cases; six
failed cases remain visible. More seriously, a development spatial audit found
that changing the reference mesh from 4 to 6 cells changes edge-drop motion by
12.13 and 29.72 mm. Narrow support contact is poorly sampled by mesh vertices.
Smaller time steps do not fix this geometry error. Thus the numbers above show
prediction against a frozen discrete simulator, not validated physical truth.
Inferred friction can compensate for numerical error, so accurate fitted motion
does not establish correct material/friction recovery or calibrated uncertainty.

What remains preserved and what is separate
Independent audits recomputed 3502 main checks and 1564 nominal-baseline checks,
with zero discrepancies. All 347 recorded v11/v12 files retain their hashes.
The public SoftVTBench and PhysTwin dataset results from v12 are unchanged; this
study does not establish a new improvement on those public benchmarks.
Three video cases were fixed before outcomes and retain failures. Playback is
4 x slower with common world axes, a moving camera and no deformation exaggeration.

A new surface-contact extension is being developed separately. It integrates
material surface patches and distributes equal/opposite forces and torques
through the actual FEM interpolation. New reference meshes and contact sample
spacing must pass fixed development consistency checks before a fresh frozen
test. Eight new parameter combinations were declared before generation. See
reports/coupled-surface-v13; a design declaration is not a completed result.

Scope
Controlled same-constitutive-family simulation transfer; known initial shape,
density, gravity, scene geometry and future tool paths. Material combinations
are within the fitted range. Interaction types differ from the calibration
motion, but the four scene families were used in numerical development. No
claim of unseen-category generalization, material-range extrapolation,
calibrated uncertainty, active probing superiority or real-time speed follows.
