COUPLED CONTACT v13: INDEPENDENT PROTOCOL AUDIT
Prepared before v13 reserved outcomes; 2026-10-30

Decision
Prioritize a small cross-interaction prediction study over a large collection of
self-reconstruction episodes. Fit an object's response from position observations
of squeezing, then predict curved-obstacle push, edge drop and sliding from tool
poses and known scene geometry. Keep the v12 benchmark evidence read-only. New
simulation results extend the evidence; they cannot improve the numerical scores
of the archived SoftVTBench/PhysTwin tables by themselves.

Why this study matters
The prior coupled prototype has equal/opposite sphere contact and a useful
refinement diagnostic, but it is a 27-node spring lattice with discrete node
collision. The benchmark implementation freezes shape after release, while the
elastic heightfield diagnostic does not establish general wall/curved contact.
The prior SoftVT improvement is small relative to rigid/contact baselines. The
new study must show measurable shape change and transfer of inferred response
between interactions, not only correct translation or fitted trajectory replay.

Recommended compact split (freeze the exact manifest before test generation)
A practical design is 4 development object/parameter combinations, 4 validation
combinations, and 8 reserved combinations. Each has one fit squeeze trajectory
and three held-out interactions: curved push, edge drop, lateral slide. That is
16 fit plus 48 forecast episodes, with 24 reserved forecasts. Smaller counts are
acceptable if declared before outcomes; more objects are preferable to more
nearly identical trajectory seeds. If runtime allows, a second amplitude or
approach direction per reserved interaction is useful but must be declared first.

Vary elastic response across at least three visibly different stiffness levels;
use at least two friction levels and two attachment/support arrangements. Include
both free and fixed-patch objects in development. Reserve combinations of these
factors, contact locations, obstacle curvature and trajectory direction; avoid
claiming unseen interaction families if the algorithm has been tuned on those
families in development. The precise claim should be: the object's parameters
are fitted using squeeze observations only, and its response transfers to new
interaction trajectories/geometries. A stronger held-out-family claim requires
one family entirely absent from development and validation outcomes.

Make gravity known and constant in the first study. Geometry, reference size,
density/mass, attachment *locations* and robot command trajectories are permitted
inputs if declared and supplied equally to all methods. Hide elastic stiffness,
friction and attachment compliance from inference. Hiding all mass, stiffness,
friction, gravity and attachment simultaneously needlessly creates an
unidentifiable inverse problem. Unknown gravity can be a later explicit ablation.
Boundary conditions include body gravity, moving contact surfaces and attachment
constraints; they are not only supports or external force labels.

Input and target isolation
For each object, fitting receives rest/reference geometry, the observed squeeze
surface/node positions and timestamps, squeeze tool poses and known geometry.
No reaction forces, stress, true material parameters, true contact labels or
reserved target object positions enter fitting or model selection. Fit/validation
within an observed squeeze may use a fixed chronological 70/30 split; rerun full
squeeze fitting only after hyperparameters are selected. Global hyperparameters
are chosen on the development/validation objects, never on reserved objects'
forecast targets. Each reserved object's squeeze is permitted test-time context.

A forecast receives its initial measured configuration and velocity (or a declared
stationary reset), known static geometry and future PRESCRIBED tool positions.
It must not receive future trajectories of freely moving bodies: those positions
are part of the prediction target. Oracle initial velocities must be labeled and
matched across methods; prefer rest initialization or an equally available short
pre-action observation. The future free-body position cannot masquerade as an
input trajectory. Separate serialized predictor payloads from scorer targets,
record hashes, and verify fitting/scoring do not share target-containing objects.

An object reset between interactions is allowed: this is cross-interaction
parameter transfer, not a continuous long rollout. Record resets, object-frame
transforms and correspondences explicitly. Use a proper rotation for the rigid
motion decomposition, and report deformation errors after rigid alignment in
addition to world-coordinate errors.

Nontrivial scene requirements
Squeeze: opposing position-driven plates, nonzero relaxation/hold and release;
include tangential offset in development if friction is to be inferred.
Curved push: a side contact with an actual curved boundary, not a heightfield-only
substitute; no silhouette-through-wall shortcut. Use an unseen contact location
or curvature in reserved cases.
Edge drop: free flight under gravity followed by off-centre contact, rotation,
compression and rebound/settling. Show both pre-contact and post-contact error.
Slide: lateral prescribed motion with normal load variation, visible stick/slip
or separation; avoid a trajectory with no frictional contact at all.
At least one scene must contain a freely moving rigid body whose translation and
rotation respond to the soft body. A prescribed gripper exchanges momentum with
an external actuator and does not alone demonstrate two-way free-body coupling.

Reference and numerical gates
Use an independent implementation or at least different mesh/resolution plus
finer integration for reference trajectories. Same-code, same-grid hidden-
parameter fitting is valuable controlled system identification but must be labeled
as such. Resolution variation is not independent validation of the constitutive
law. If only a spring-lattice reference is feasible, report that scope clearly;
do not relabel spring parameters as validated continuum Young's modulus.

Qualify development scenes before freezing: finite trajectories, noninverted
volume elements if tetrahedral, sustained nonrigid deformation after removing
rigid motion, contact actually exercised, and unilateral separation. Check normal
reaction is nonnegative, friction is dissipative, and pair contact applies equal
and opposite forces and torques at a common contact point. Check isolated linear
and angular momentum without gravity/support/drives; with those enabled, account
for external impulse and work rather than demanding conservation.

Check timestep refinement on the ENTIRE position trajectory at common times,
not only the final state: three levels with decreasing errors to the finest and
an error small relative to the effect being studied. Include peak penetration,
contact timing and nonrigid response in refinement. Penetration tolerance should
be relative to object scale and tighter than the deformation effect. Record mesh
refinement or explicitly limit claims to the discrete model. A useful initial
development target is maximum penetration below 2% of object width and refinement
coordinate RMS below 0.5% of width, with monotone improvement. These are proposed
engineering thresholds, not already attained results; freeze adopted thresholds
before reserved generation. Do not silently drop failed cases. Apply a declared
reference-failure rule, publish full counts, and never exclude cases because the
new predictor loses. Low-deformation reserved cases remain in the full aggregate
and can be separately stratified using predeclared thresholds.

Comparisons and ablations
1. Rigid body with the same scene/contact geometry and equally fitted pose: tests
whether the task actually needs elasticity.
2. Position-conditioned ridge predictor fitted to the identical squeeze context;
fixed regularization chosen on development. It cannot be expected to infer a new
obstacle without geometry input; disclose that difference or include matched
geometric features. Do not call it a physics method.
3. Current v12 frozen-shape-contact method, adapted only through an explicit input
adapter, labeled as v12 adaptation. If interface/scene limitations prevent a fair
run, report unsupported cases rather than an invented v12 surrogate.
4. Material/contact model with nominal parameters versus inferred parameters.
5. No-contact, frozen-shape and one-way-contact ablations, retaining the same
inferred parameters and scene settings where possible.
6. True-parameter forward-model oracle, diagnostic only. If this fails, parameter
inference cannot repair the physical model reliably.
7. Official PhysTwin implementation only with matched observation/context budgets,
permitted inputs and declared adaptation. A newly authored spring baseline is
NOT PhysTwin. If a native run cannot be completed, the study can still establish
mechanism/ablation evidence; it must not claim a PhysTwin benchmark victory.

Evaluation and uncertainty
Primary: equal-object then equal-interaction aggregate world-coordinate error and
rigid-aligned shape error, in physical units. Also free rigid-body pose error,
contact penetration/failure counts, and pre-contact/post-contact breakdown.
Report each interaction family and every case, wins/losses, and paired changes;
bootstrap objects, not correlated frames, if confidence intervals are used.
Do not pool unrelated dataset metrics. A benchmark preservation audit is separate:
hash the old predictions/sources and compare a fixed replay subset unchanged.

Squeeze alone may not identify friction or fixture compliance. A narrow optimum
is not a calibrated posterior. Profile/ensemble diagnostic alternatives can show
ambiguity; describe ranges as hypotheses unless coverage is validated. Compare
true-parameter predictions, best-fit predictions and alternative near-fit
hypotheses on unseen interactions to separate forward error from ambiguity.
Do not select the best hypothesis using held-out future error. Active probing
remains a later study unless a separate frozen observation/action protocol fits
within the campaign.

Audit checkpoints
A. Manifest, inputs and numerical thresholds frozen before reserved outputs.
B. Development-only simulation qualification and oracle check.
C. Model/hyperparameter freeze, fit termination rules and failure policy recorded.
D. Independent leakage audit before reserved scoring.
E. All cases scored once; any subsequent change is a new exploratory version.
F. Fixed representative videos include one of each interaction, any serious
failure and both world/rigid-aligned views, using identical scales and cameras.
G. Final claims distinguish controlled simulation evidence, archived public
benchmark preservation, native benchmark reruns, and unimplemented capabilities.

Decision recommendation
Proceed with the compact cross-interaction study and its matched simple baselines.
Do not delay genuine contact/deformation qualification to fit a larger neural
network. Preserve prior results literally. Prioritize a successful true-parameter
forward oracle and a real two-way free-body scene before expensive inference.

Each object has four forecast episodes: slide, edge-drop, curved-push and contact
with a freely moving rigid sphere. Thus 14 calibration +56 forecasts total,

combine curved-push and moving-sphere into curved push against a free rigid sphere
and declare 42 forecasts before generation. A static curved obstacle plus an
unrelated tiny sphere qualification must not be described as the same combined

contact offsets +/-0.15 width and opposite/lateral directions. Specify each
trajectory as normalized tool-pose keyframes before numerical rendering. Use the
same fixed keyframes per interaction across methods. Geometry changes are inputs;
reserved target geometry trajectories are not. At least two reserved objects

unseen combinations and geometry/path transfer, not material-range extrapolation.

interaction-family test. Report the two E-extrapolation objects separately without
replacing the all-eight-object aggregate.

Additional concrete qualification rules
- Numerical roundoff gate for isolated pairwise force/torque cancellation:
  relative residual <=1e-10 in float64 against nonzero force/torque scales, with
  an absolute tolerance also declared for zero-load cases.
- All sampled and integrated reference tetrahedral determinants positive; log
  minimum J, not just sampled-frame J. If a finite safety margin is used, freeze
  it and distinguish it from the mathematical noninversion condition J>0.
- Three timesteps h, h/2, h/4; require medium-versus-fine trajectory coordinate
  RMS <=0.005*width and <=0.8 times coarse-versus-fine RMS. This 0.8 ratio is a
  practical improvement gate, not an assertion of a solver's formal order.
- Maximum reference penetration <=0.02*width. Report phase and time of the max.
- Development scenes should exhibit max rigid-aligned coordinate RMS deformation
  >=0.02*width in at least squeeze and one forecast interaction. This is a scene
  design target, not a post-hoc exclusion rule for reserved cases.
- Mesh refinement uses common reference surface probes/correspondence. Report its
  error; if not demonstrably converged, state discrete-model transfer rather than
  continuum validation. Do not hide coarse-predictor discretization error inside
  inferred material differences: show the true-parameter oracle on that grid.

These are proposed frozen gates, not empirical claims. The parent may select an
alternative before outcomes and must retain the signed final values/protocol.

FINAL ROOT-SPECIFIED NUMERICAL MATRIX AUDIT
This supersedes the normalized matrix proposal above. Proposed alternatives are
retained as decision history, not simultaneous experiment specifications.

Object dimensions 0.08 x 0.06 x 0.05 m; density 1000 kg/m^3; Poisson ratio 0.3.
Development: (E Pa,mu) = (6000,0.35), (3000,0.10).
Validation Cartesian product: E={4500,9000} Pa; mu={0.20,0.55} (4 objects).
Reserved Cartesian product: E={3500,5500,8000,11000} Pa; mu={0.15,0.60}
(8 objects). Four forecast families per object, squeeze+shear calibration only.
No material-range extrapolation claim: all reserved material/friction values lie
within declared simulator/material development envelopes. This is unseen
combinations and cross-interaction transfer conditioned on each object's squeeze.
The E=11000 reserved values exceed the specific validation E=9000 maximum but
fall within declared E=12000 engineering envelope; describe the actual split,
not a blanket unseen-material claim.

Frozen candidate grid recommendation:
E={2000,3000,4500,6500,9000,13000,18000} Pa;
mu={0.0,0.2,0.4,0.6,0.8}; Cartesian product 35 hypotheses.
All reference E and mu fall strictly inside the search ranges. The true values
are usually not grid members, preventing a trivial lookup of exact labels.
Choose by the declared position loss, deterministic tie-break, equal observation
budget and identical solver settings. No test-driven grid refinement. Uniform
local refinement is possible only if bounds, iterations, fit loss and termination
are declared before freeze and applied to every object. The finite grid alone is
a defensible, reproducible starting method; parameter quantization is a reported
limitation, not a reason to manually repair failed test objects.

Boundary/identifiability audit requirements
1. Report selected-grid edge hits (E=2000 or18000, mu=0 or0.8), best-vs-second-best
   calibration loss, and the parameter span of hypotheses within a fixed declared
   loss tolerance. Edge hits indicate search/model mismatch, not proof that the
   true parameter lies beyond the grid. Do not expand the grid using test errors.
2. Friction excitation must actually visit its force-limiting regime. For a
   min(c*speed,mu*normal) law, slow tangential motion can remain in the viscous
   branch for all mu; a shear command alone does not establish identifiability.
   Log Coulomb-cap-active contact fraction, tangential travel and stick/slip in
   development. Adjust the development excitation before the protocol freeze.
3. Compare calibration losses across the mu dimension at fixed E. Flat profiles
   mean the observation cannot identify friction. Preserve alternate plausible
   hypotheses; do not invent confidence from a single argmin. A predicted motion
   can still transfer well even if parameters remain ambiguous.
4. With normal squeezing prescribed, stiffness affects reaction forces directly,
   while observed positions may be partly imposed by plates. Observe free lateral
   bulging, relaxation or release transient as well as contacted nodes. A model
   that observes only forced node coordinates can fit all stiffnesses equally.
5. Keep mass, Poisson ratio, damping convention, attachment geometry and contact
   penalty settings known/fixed across inference and reference as declared. If
   coarse/fine dynamics use different effective damping or contact stiffness,
   document it as model mismatch. Do not interpret fitted E as uniquely recovered
   material stiffness in that case.
6. Primary qualification thresholds should normalize by the minimum object
   dimension L=0.05 m for conservatism: maximum penetration <=1.0 mm,
   medium-to-fine coordinate trajectory RMS <=0.25 mm, and development peak
   rigid-aligned coordinate RMS >=1.0 mm in squeeze and >=one forecast family.
   Refinement ratio <=0.8 and positivity/reciprocity rules remain as above.
