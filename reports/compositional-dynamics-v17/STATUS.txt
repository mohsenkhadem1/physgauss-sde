V17 DYNAMIC EXTENSION: COMPUTATION AND INDEPENDENT AUDITS COMPLETE
2 November 2026. Release contents: audited results, videos and paper.

What we built

The new examples include compression and recovery with 22.7% material shortening,
a finite 80 g sphere impact with bending and rebound, and a viscous drop that
bounces and settles. Stronger simulations that failed numerical checks remain
recorded. The compression cycle passed its own mesh, time-step and quadrature
checks; that qualification does not automatically apply to every example.

The fresh benchmark contains four material/contact worlds. Each method observes
a 10 mm calibration drop, then predicts 40 mm and 80 mm drops without refitting.
Inputs are known geometry, mass and gravity plus visible oracle 3D tracks. Hidden
material/contact labels and future motion are withheld. This is not end-to-end
inference from raw video.

We used the actual PhysTwin author implementation for fitting, physics and
Gaussian rendering. Its recipe was selected using development calibration only,
before the fresh worlds were generated. Twelve native Newton development runs
are reported separately as numerical controls, not a matched superiority claim.

Fresh results

Our method completed all eight forecasts. PhysTwin completed six; an empty graph
during fitting on world 3 left its two forecasts unavailable. Both failures remain
in the reports, coverage counts and videos.

On the same six successful paired forecasts across three worlds:
- Motion vector RMS: 1.146 mm for ours versus 13.771 mm for native PhysTwin.
- Rigid-aligned deformation RMS: 1.085 mm versus 2.936 mm.
- New-view foreground PSNR: 18.734 dB versus 12.842 dB.
- New-view foreground SSIM: 0.7473 versus 0.5770.
- Silhouette IoU: 0.9562 versus 0.7950.
- Joint-visible depth RMSE: 5.478 mm versus 14.557 mm.

These are conditional paired comparisons, not an all-eight paired mean. Both
methods start with the same learned Gaussian appearance. Image differences
include motion prediction and Gaussian transport jointly. All 2,480 image slots
are recorded: 2,170 successful images and 310 unavailable native images.

The shared fits used 192 coarse and 254 of at most 256 fine evaluations, plus 216
cached responses. One fine search stopped early. Only one of sixteen optimizers
reported convergence; completed forecasts do not imply converged material
estimates. No model tuning followed the fresh test scores.

Reference reliability

All 20 reference simulations completed and passed their individual energy,
Jacobian and penetration checks. All four time-step comparisons passed, but all
four spatial comparisons failed. The benchmark therefore uses finite-resolution
synthetic targets, not continuum-qualified truth.

A post-hoc diagnostic tested the same frozen predictions against existing n4,
n6 and half-step n6 references on both fixed 80 mm cases. Our lower world and
shape errors persisted at every checked resolution. Absolute errors changed
with resolution; the primary scores and failed spatial qualification are retained.

Interpretation

This is a promising matched-observation dynamic prediction improvement over the
selected native PhysTwin configuration, with better predicted-view quality on
paired cases. It does not establish broad SOTA performance, real-world accuracy,
unseen interaction or geometry generalization, calibrated uncertainty or active
probing. Raw datasets and checkpoints stay local. CPU and shared GPUs 0-2 were
used without stopping unrelated jobs or using GPU 3 for this campaign.
