GAUSSIAN BOUNDARY FLOW: FIRST IMPLEMENTATION AND TEST RESULTS
4 November 2026

What works
A conditional flow model infers distributions over hidden stiffness, attachment
strength/location, load magnitude/location and damping from observed motion.
Each sample drives the existing mechanics, and the existing Gaussian renderer
produces images. Prediction uses forward integration, with no inverse refitting.

The finer model uses 64 physical nodes, 8192 training worlds and 128 held-out
worlds. Force programs are changed after the observed prefix. Coordinate RMSE:
                       Conditional flow    Neural regressor    Linear regressor
Continued loading         0.198 mm             0.358 mm             1.507 mm
Load release              0.189 mm             0.338 mm             1.457 mm
Triangular load           0.196 mm             0.354 mm             1.501 mm
Force reversal            0.206 mm             0.353 mm             1.490 mm
Position-driven lift      0.189 mm             0.338 mm
The lift supplies a 6 mm upward attachment displacement, not a fitted force.

Across all four force programs, paired bootstrap intervals for improvement over
the neural regressor exclude zero. These are development baselines, not PhysTwin
or another state-of-the-art comparison.

Video input and rendering
The initial coarse prototype also consumes synthetic RGB videos (known appearance,
geometry and cameras), using a train-only PCA encoder. Future motion error is
0.96-0.99 mm, versus 3.00-3.42 mm for its regression baseline. Missing intermediate
frames score 53.81 dB versus 49.11 dB for simple image blending. These rendering
results belong to the coarse prototype, not the finer physical model.

New information and uncertainty
One additional view reduces error from 0.205 to 0.093 mm in 32 fresh cases.
The selected-view policy ties the fixed view; no policy superiority is established.
When observations come from a 125-node simulator, prediction error is 0.250 mm,
but raw nominal 90% coordinate coverage drops to 80.2%. Separate finer-domain
calibration gives 93.75% whole-trajectory coverage in 64 fresh cases, with average
interval width 1.40 mm. This calibrates predictions, not material or force labels.

Numerics and limitations
Temporal refinement changes motion by 0.080 mm. CPU/GPU agree within 0.2 micrometres.
Spatial refinement remains material: 64->125 nodes changes corner motion by
0.336 mm in an eight-case check. The model is linear elastic and is not qualified
for extreme deformation or large rotations. Known initial geometry, mass, cameras,
force direction and future command are still required. Contact and exact-boundary
operators have unit tests, but mixed boundary-type discovery and coupled drops/
impacts are not yet part of the learned rollout. Real-video adaptation remains.
