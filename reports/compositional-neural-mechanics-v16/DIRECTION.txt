Compositional neural mechanics — implementation direction
Date: 2026-11-02
Status: design decision and staged implementation specification; no training run
or new performance claim. Prior v14/v15 results and frozen inputs stay unchanged.

Primary research question
Can globally shared bulk and interface laws, with scene-specific latent fields,
predict interactions for unseen combinations of material, geometry and support,
and improve after a small amount of new scene evidence?

Primary claim to test: compositional physical prediction and identification.
Secondary claim to test: useful observation acquisition. Adaptive superiority
is not assumed; the v15 global fixed two-bend control remains a required control.

Representation
1. Bodies: separate object identity, reference geometry, mass/density and state.
   Deformable material coordinates X travel with each object. Rigid bodies have
   SE(3) pose, velocity and inertia; they are not approximated by infinite stiffness.
2. Shared bulk energy W_psi(F,z_M(X)). Start with objective isotropic hyperelastic
   response and spatial heterogeneity. Stress is dW/dF. Use a constrained energy
   architecture with explicit reference-state normalization; differentiating an
   arbitrary energy network alone does not ensure stability or coercivity.
3. Shared relational interface laws indexed by body pair and candidate surface
   patches. Inputs include local geometry, normal, gap and relative motion.
   Scene-specific z_I describes attachment/compliance/friction parameters.
   Known world anchors and prescribed tools are explicit environment entities.
4. Gravity and other body loads enter the mechanics equations separately.
   Distributed surface tractions and prescribed kinematics retain their physical
   roles. Calling all of them a learned boundary field obscures identifiability.
5. One mechanics layer composes all terms, enforces admissible contact and returns
   equilibrium/dynamic state, reaction wrench, contact state and residual checks.
6. Rendering consumes this same state. Appearance Gaussians, if used, are attached
   to material coordinates; they do not receive an independent motion correction
   that hides mechanical prediction errors. Gaussian kernels can separately
   parameterize latent fields, but they are not themselves constitutive laws.

Interface semantics
Use one shared interface representation with explicit physically distinct modes:
compliant bilateral attachment, unilateral nonadhesive contact, and frictional
contact with tangential history. Adhesion is deferred. Ordinary contact separates
without tension; attachments can transmit tension. A learned gate must not
silently turn every contact into a spring. Coupled forces act on both bodies.
A friction-cone inequality alone is insufficient: include stick/slip evolution
and dissipation. Store history in a consistent interface frame.

Inputs and outputs
Inputs: RGB-D/camera poses or known geometry in the first controlled experiment,
observed state history, prescribed tool pose trajectory and time, known gravity,
and optional measured reaction observations as a separately reported modality.
Control inputs are positions/orientations, not hidden true force labels.
Outputs: object poses, material deformation/velocity, reaction forces/torques,
contact/separation states, predictive distributions and images of the same state.
Rigid motion and nonrigid deformation are scored separately.

Learning versus scene adaptation
Train shared law weights across training scenes. At test time freeze those weights
and infer a low-dimensional scene latent posterior from permitted observations.
An encoder may initialize latents, but must not infer mechanics from undeclared
ground-truth labels or visually encoded material IDs. Appearance randomization
and geometry-only controls test this leakage explicitly.
Use a small direct latent optimization/ensemble baseline before adding amortized
transformer inference. Avoid unidentifiable redundant per-element parameters.
A posterior mean of physical responses need not be an equilibrium solution;
individual hypotheses and their renderings remain mechanically consistent.

Implementation stages
A. Shared bulk law plus spatial compliant attachments; existing verified FEM is
   the initial global solver. Multiple reference geometries and material fields,
   held material/support combinations, and position-controlled indentation,
   bending and unloading. Fixed-law fitted FEM and a direct displacement network
   are mandatory baselines. First evaluate with oracle latents, then inferred
   latents, to separate forward transfer from inverse ambiguity.
B. One explicit rigid tool/floor contacting a deformable object; normal contact
   first, then a bounded frictional interface family. Reuse existing contact code
   only where numerical qualification supports the chosen regime. No open-ended
   friction tuning; advance to the full benchmark once the declared contact
   scenario passes its mechanical checks. Include rigid reaction and momentum
   accounting where bodies move.
C. Unknown material/support/contact explanations jointly. Separate held-out
   combinations from held-out geometries and interaction directions. Test latent
   updates with one/two fixed or random probes before claiming adaptive benefit.
D. Warm-start neural operator only after shared-law composition works. Correct
   using residual/contact criteria and a solver fallback, not a fixed iteration
   count presented as a guarantee. General soft-soft contact and viscoelastic
   memory are subsequent extensions, not simultaneous first milestones.

Constitutive scope
Use at least two distinct elastic response families in the generator/evaluation
to test more than fitting a Young's modulus inside a known fixed equation. The
initial learned energy may be an admissible residual around an analytic law;
compare with a fitted analytic-family baseline of similar latent capacity.
Generalization within trained law families, held-out combinations and held-out
law families are different claims. Isotropic inputs do not confer anisotropic
generalization: structural tensors and anisotropic training evidence are required.
Hypernetwork conditioning must preserve architecture constraints for every latent.

Evaluation and uncertainty
Reserve whole combinations and geometries before training; split by scene rather
than video frame. Reuse local benchmark adapters to test retention of prior motion
performance, but do not invent unavailable force/contact ground truth on those
datasets. Controlled simulations provide the physical labels.
Report world-space motion, rigid-aligned deformation, reaction wrench, penetration,
contact transitions and clean-render agreement separately. Compare primary
predictive means and physically valid individual hypotheses explicitly.
Keep fixed camera-independent and camera-conditioned probe controls, random probe
pairs, visual uncertainty and physical-risk selection at matched observation
budgets. Choose risk-design actions from training/development only; reserved
response outcomes must never enter acquisition or model selection.
Model numerical/observation discrepancy and check interval coverage on independent
calibration scenes before active-design claims. More ensemble particles or a
larger neural network do not by themselves fix v15 undercoverage.
With zero loads and purely position-controlled static observations, common
stiffness scaling remains visually unidentifiable. Additional views cannot fix
that. Reactions or known loads/dynamics can provide extra information, with mass,
density and other confounding factors explicitly declared.

Repository integration
Add new implementation under a separate compositional_neural_mechanics namespace.
Preserve experiments/gaussian_operator.py and gaussian_modal_operator.py as
baselines: current modules explicitly describe fixed-reference direct trajectory
models, so changing their parameter vector alone does not create shared laws.
Reuse v15 observation contracts, selected-only broker, response metrics, numerical
qualification and audits; reuse gaussian_active_design risk machinery with a
new qualified latent/model interface. Contact v13 is reusable infrastructure,
not blanket evidence that a new learned interface is qualified.

Literature positioning checked for this direction
PhysHO already combines MPM with residual neural constitutive models:
 https://suezjiang.github.io/physho/
PhysGS already maintains Bayesian property beliefs on Gaussian splats:
 https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2026/html/Chopra_PhysGS_Bayesian-Inferred_Gaussian_Splatting_for_Physical_Property_Estimation_CVPR_2026_paper.html
NGFF already learns force fields from Gaussian scene representations:
 https://neuralgaussianforcefield.github.io/
These overlaps rule out claiming the individual ingredients as new. The proposed
contribution is a hypothesis about transferable composition and intervention-
based identification; establishing novelty requires a fuller method-level
comparison, not the working name ICNM alone. Other unspecified references in the
attached discussion have not been independently verified in this direction note.
