BRANCH CONTINUATION — INDEPENDENT PRACTICAL DECISION AUDIT
2026-10-30. Diagnosis only; frozen148case feasibility campaign unchanged.

DECISION
Proceed with a bounded solver study on development and the known failed zebra.
First try SAME-energy continuation; do not add empirical force/material complexity
until determining whether the existing solver is following a stable branch.
A topology guard cannot detect the documented large positive-J pose jump.

ORDERED EXPERIMENT
1. At the pre-jump solution and candidate post-jump equilibrium, compute full
   original-energy residual, signedJ and the smallest eigenvalue of the symmetric
   unmodified Hessian (report any pin/gauge contribution separately). A tiny
   residual with a negative physical-curvature mode is a saddle, not a stable
   quasistatic solution. Verify energy derivatives against directional differences
   on representative states; do not confuse an internally shifted Newton Hessian
   used for solving with the actual energy Hessian used for stability diagnosis.
2. Interpolate only the prescribed controls between adjacent frames and solve
   from the last accepted state with fixed subdivision levels1,2,4,8,16 (bounded).
   Preserve physical energy/material values, reference mesh and geometry. Record
   every rejected substep, residual, minJ, energy and displacement. Controller
   interpolation must respect the representation (proper rotation interpolation
   if interpolating rigid poses rather than declared nodal position controls).
3. Check path refinement: do endpoints/path approach a stable result as steps
   shrink? Do not pick the subdivision with the best target error. A fixed
   numerically selected rule may accept a solution only using convergence,
   feasibility and stability evidence, not true trajectory discrepancy.
4. If stable branch disappears or repeated refinement does not settle, flag a
   genuine branch transition/instability. Move to an explicitly DIFFERENT dynamic
   model with mass/inertia and dissipative terms, not an undisclosed displacement
   clamp or target-driven force. A large snap-through can be physically real;
   a rule demanding displacement smaller than tool motion would suppress it.

WHAT COUNTS AS A NUMERICAL FIX
Homotopy/trust-region continuation on the unchanged energy may change the selected
local equilibrium. Describe that branch-selection rule explicitly; it is not a
proof of the unique physical branch. Check force residual on the final original
energy, not only a proximal surrogate. Time-step refinement should reduce solver
sensitivity, and all original constitutive/attachment parameters remain frozen.
Dynamic/proximal regularization is a model change if its penalty remains in the
accepted equations. Calling it numerical only requires demonstrating convergence
as the artificial regularization is removed while the original residual remains
small. Physical inertia should instead be labelled dynamics, with declared mass,
time-step and dissipative assumptions; it cannot be claimed inferred merely from
an improved fit. Curvature sign alone cannot establish dynamic stability under
all contact constraints, but it identifies obvious unconstrained saddle outputs.

PRACTICAL GO / NO-GO FOR PROMOTION
GO as a separately labelled numerical candidate if a fixed input-only rule:
- avoids saddle acceptance and produces finite positive-J states;
- converges under the predeclared control refinement, including transition frames;
- retains original-energy residual tolerance and unchanged fitted parameters;
- behaves correctly on simple rigid-motion, gradual loading and known snap-through
  or loss-of-equilibrium qualification cases (do not force every transition smooth);
- reports the same rule on ALL8solid PT cases, all original prefixes/suffixes,
  failures, degraded cases and reference comparisons, not only the failed zebra.
NO-GO as a reliable physical cure if it only delays the large jump, requires
case-specific target-calibrated damping, or attains small geometric error at a
large unreported original residual. Then retain it as a failed/useful diagnostic.
Bound the first study (e.g.60–90minutes) and decide from numerical convergence,
not serial new benchmark experiments. Frozen148case processing stays unchanged.

EVALUATION / SERIAL HOLDOUT POLICY
The zebra was selected because it failed; success on it is diagnostic, not an
unbiased performance estimate. Original106 and fresh42 phase labels remain as
published for their respective frozen models. A later branch change evaluated
on already inspected cases must not inherit the earlier fresh label automatically.

running. Publish every phase and retain failed methods/guarded tradeoffs in paper.
No best-of-old-versus-new per-sequence future-error selection. Any prefix selector
would need a separately refitted shorter-prefix protocol and frozen candidate set.
Report displacement/velocity continuity and original-energy stability alongside
accuracy; do not use the continuity diagnostic alone to declare physical success.
