ELASTIC CONTACT DIAGNOSTIC — DEVELOPMENT ONLY

Scope
A separate alternative to the frozen primary reduced contact method. This
experiment integrates Neo-Hookean deformation during contact instead of freezing
shape at separation. It is NOT a primary benchmark result or a general contact
convergence claim. No primary case was used to select these configurations.

Implementation
scripts/contact_benchmark_v12/scene_fem.py reuses the fitted material parameters
and corrected DynamicFEM. Initial static scene meshes supply lagged per-node
support heights. The old reversible tangential spring is disabled; an optional
regularized dissipative friction potential uses lagged normal forces. The solver
has exact energy derivatives and Hessians. Failed frame integration remains
explicit, and timestep retries roll back state. The optional flag
-- conceptually disable_field_on_release=True -- removes the learned world
potential only during detached inertial solves, preserving its static behavior.

These are vertically queried layered heightfields, not full signed-distance
contact, continuous collision detection, or IPC. Side-wall impacts and moving
rigid partners are not modeled. Contact forces act on elastic shape in this
candidate, unlike the primary reduced pose-contact variant.

Fixed development experiment
SoftVTBench spatial-soft task0 demo0, release frames 107 through 114 inclusive
(eight frames). The existing full-prefix fit is reused without refitting or
changing material parameters. Initial state and velocity are taken from the
predicted states immediately before release. Source:
runs/contact-benchmark-v12/base/full/spatial-soft-task0-demo0/
No future observed shape is used for prediction; development truth is used only
to score the completed forecasts.

Results (coordinate RMSE in millimetres over these eight frames)
Existing base: 18.6996.
Scene contact with learned field and friction 0.3:
  5 / 10 / 20 / 40 / 80 substeps: 16.9749 / 17.0716 / 17.5351 /
  17.6391 / 17.2483.
Scene contact with field removed during release, friction 0.3:
  20 / 80 / 160 substeps: 17.5542 / 17.3301 / 16.8440.
Scene contact with field removed and friction zero:
  20 / 80 / 160 substeps: 16.8433 / 16.3009 / 16.0446.
All of these runs finished without failed frames. Successful integration alone
does not establish accurate or converged physical dynamics.

Numerical diagnosis
With learned field and friction 0.3, consecutive timestep refinements
5->10, 10->20, 20->40, and 40->80 have maximum per-frame geometry differences
0.9997, 2.4411, 1.5296, and 2.3101 mm. They are not monotonically convergent.

Removing the learned field alone does not cure the sensitivity: 80->160 reaches
5.7921 mm at the final frame. Removing friction as well reduces the final
80->160 discrepancy to 0.474 mm, although the peak transient is still 2.344 mm.
Contact is first present at sampled frame 108, following release at frame 107;
refinement differences increase substantially during contact. These ablations
implicate contact transients and lagged-friction sensitivity. They do not uniquely
separate raster switching, stiff elastic response, and friction discretization.

With the support floor moved far below the object, field disabled, and friction
zero, the finite-element mass-weighted centroid matches analytical implicit-Euler
gravity motion to less than 1e-9 mm. Its remaining offset from continuous ballistic
motion is the expected first-order timestep error. The maximum 80->160 full-mesh
geometry difference is 0.688 mm. This rules out an artificial net free-flight
force in this tested configuration; it does not prove internal-mode convergence.
Free-flight error against the actual landing sequence is intentionally large:
this is a force-isolation experiment, not a candidate landing predictor.

Tests
Six tests in tests/test_contact_scene_fem.py check analytic gradient/Hessian
consistency, no teleportation onto overhead supports, free-flight refinement,
unchanged caller attachment inputs, genuine elastic compression at floor impact,
and exact preservation of static field behavior when release suppression is
requested. Together with object-frame and prefix-isolation tests, 25 tests pass.
The synthetic impact test shortens a tetrahedron edge from 40 to approximately
26.8 mm; this verifies actual shape response rather than rigid-only movement.

Recommendation
Keep the frozen primary method unchanged. Before promoting fully elastic scene
contact, resolve friction/contact timestep sensitivity with a controlled impact
and sliding qualification, then verify convergence on multiple development
objects. A more continuous geometric contact representation and consistently
resolved friction are stronger next targets than treating one favorable error
value as proof of reliable dynamics.
