CAMPAIGN v12: OBJECT MOTION, DEFORMATION AND CONTACT
Completed study; all 148 selected forecasts available. Raw datasets stay local.

What changed, in plain language
We let the model distinguish moving a whole object from changing its shape.
We add known static scene surfaces so a released object can fall, separate,
and meet a support. We check that shape corrections do not turn mesh elements
inside out. Finally, we compare three explanations using only the beginning
of each recording: rigid motion, the existing deformable model, and a refitted
deformable model without its global Gaussian potential. The short-prefix
validation chooses a model; the future object motion is used only for scoring.
Future tool positions are supplied. This is conditional forecasting.

Main results (lower is better; equal sequence weighting)
Dataset        Cases   Starting v11    Guarded extension    New selector
SoftVTBench      140      23.85 mm         21.15 mm             20.90 mm
PhysTwin           8      48.91 mm         46.39 mm             33.02 mm
SoftVT uses coordinate RMSE; PhysTwin uses manual-marker mean error. Do not
pool these metrics. These all-case selector results are POST-AUDIT EXPLORATORY
and include development sequences, not a new independent generalization test.

The largest repair is the zebra lift: 131.00 -> 38.05 mm. It contributes about
87% of the PhysTwin gain over the guarded version. All four other unchanged
PhysTwin cases retain their previous predictions exactly; four improve.
On SoftVT, the small incremental average gain hides 41 improvements, 61
regressions and 38 ties versus the guarded version. Against starting v11,
139/140 SoftVT and 7/8 PhysTwin cases improve. Earlier results remain archived.

Strong comparisons remain important
Rigid motion with the same scene contact has lower SoftVT mean: 20.51 mm.
Official PhysTwin has lower error on all eight PhysTwin cases: 21.14 mm mean.
The simple position-ridge baseline also beats our PhysTwin mean: 29.70 mm.
On 120 matched nondevelopment SoftVT cases, our selector is 21.83 mm versus
35.30 mm for the locally adapted PhysTwin model plus the same contact stage.
That adaptation comparison is not the official PhysTwin benchmark ranking.

Separate independent evidence
Before designing the later selector, the guarded version was frozen and tested
on 42 additional trajectories: SoftVT 23.97 -> 21.30 mm (39/40 improvements),
PhysTwin 63.43 -> 60.40 mm (2/2). These are known object categories with new
trajectories. The later selector cannot inherit that confirmation designation.

What the audits changed
Agents independently reviewed method choices, topology, benchmarks and videos.
They found inverted elements after empirical correction and a large incorrect equilibrium
jump in the zebra case. The local-volume guard addresses the former; prefix
selection between refitted model families reduces the latter. Full benchmark
forecasting still uses a split contact approximation with static scene bodies.
A separate small soft-block/moving-sphere prototype tests two-way contact but
has not been transferred into these benchmark forecasts. A nonlinear release
diagnostic failed convergence and was not promoted.

Checks and remaining limits
629 fitting jobs completed, including 80 native GPU fits on GPUs 0-2; unrelated
jobs were left running. 92 numerical tests pass. Independent verification
recomputed 592 forecast scores and checked all 296 new fits. Twelve fixed cases
replayed selection metadata and all four forecast arrays exactly. All 148
selected forecasts are finite and satisfy the saved-frame local volume margin.
This does not guarantee equilibrium, global injectivity or timestep convergence.
Eight full-prefix and five short-prefix fits have abnormal optimizer flags.
Standalone current-FEM outputs are complete on 132/140 SoftVT and 7/8 PhysTwin;
field-free outputs on 126/140 and 8/8. Incomplete-family means are conditional.
The selected method itself is complete on every declared case.
An evaluator-only amendment handles missing marker correspondence for one
failed standalone zebra output without changing any prediction.

We have improved average prediction and numerical reliability over our starting
model. We have not established superiority to the strongest baselines, general
moving-object contact, calibrated material/boundary uncertainty, or active probing.
The next substantive step is contact that changes shape and reacts on both bodies,
followed by a new frozen evaluation on interactions not used in this campaign.
