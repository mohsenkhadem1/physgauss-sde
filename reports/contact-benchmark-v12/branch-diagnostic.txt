BOUNDED EQUILIBRIUM-BRANCH DIAGNOSIS — NO PRIMARY METHOD CHANGE

Scope
A mechanistic diagnostic on development single_lift_sloth and the already-known
single_lift_zebra failure. No future-target loss was computed to choose branches,
settings or variants. No variant is promoted to the 148-case guarded benchmark.
The eight available solid-toy PhysTwin cases are already used; these findings
are not fresh confirmation evidence.

Frozen-energy controller continuation does not fix the zebra jump
Starting from the same predicted frame51 and fitted parameters, solving frame 52
with 1/2/4/8/16 linearly interpolated commanded-position steps always reaches the
same approximately 244.834 mm mean node displacement. At 16 steps the largest change
occurs at command fraction 0.3125:154.16 mm coordinate RMS in one substep.
The smallest static Hessian eigenvalue decreases from 3.06e-5 at fraction 0.0625
to 1.29e-5 at0.25, then the solver reaches a different stable equilibrium with
minimum eigenvalue about 5.28e-6. At the final command it is 5.19e-6, with no negative
eigenvalues, residual about 1.9e-11 and minimum J 0.33439.

The previous shape's energy under the new command is 0.00815456; the new
equilibrium has energy 0.00481305. Consequently, neither an energy-decrease test
nor positive-Hessian filtering would reject this wrong-but-feasible solution.
The evidence is consistent with a branch transition of the fitted energy. It
does not prove a unique mathematical bifurcation mechanism or imply that a
physical snap of the real object occurred. The existing failure audit documents
the disagreement with recorded motion.

The independently fitted validation trajectory still fails at frame 32 under
1/4/8 continuation steps, with iteration-limit residuals 1.06e-5,5.69e-5,2.33e-5.
Those failures are preserved in the JSON records. On development sloth, static
1 versus 8 step continuation over frames 59:64 differs by only 0.0000513 mm maximum
per-frame coordinate RMS. Simple controller substepping is therefore not a
systematic repair of this fitted model's failure.

Inertia smooths the jump but does not establish a correct model
A separate implicit-inertia rollout uses the existing source mass/gravity ratio,
unchanged fitted parameters and prescribed positions. It starts from the last
observed-prefix predicted state, with velocity from the preceding prediction.
This changes the quasistatic model; density is not independently identified.

For zebra frames 46:66, 8->16 and 16->32 substeps produce maximum trajectory
coordinate-RMS differences 0.48869 mm and 0.29777 mm. The instantaneous frame 52 jump
is reduced to about 4.50 mm mean node displacement at 32 steps. However motion
continues accelerating, reaching 23.10 mm per frame by frame 65. Thus inertia may delay
motion toward the same problematic attractor; no accuracy improvement is claimed.
For development sloth frames 59:85, 8->16 differs by at most 0.04682 mm. Successful
refinement in these bounded tests does not independently validate fitted physics.

Learned world-potential ablation identifies a conditional mechanism
With the same parameters but the Gaussian world potential disabled, zebra's
static full-suffix rollout has maximum per-frame mean displacement 4.60 mm. The
1 versus 8 step paths agree to 0.000806 mm maximum coordinate RMS. Removing the old
reversible tangential support term alone instead retains a 79.72 mm frame 52 jump.
The fitted zebra Gaussian coefficient is positive 0.045996; its positive Gaussian
energy produces a repulsive force away from the field center. This unlocalized
surrogate can influence a largely unconstrained rigid mode after support changes.
These controlled model ablations implicate the world term in this example;
they are not evidence that every learned field is wrong.

Crucial development counterexample: disabling the field with unchanged sloth
parameters causes an immediate 74.39 mm first-step adjustment, versus about 1.61 mm
with the original energy. Its1 versus 8 step no-field paths still agree within
0.00180 mm. This is a changed-energy effect, not a solver-resolution problem.
Parameters fitted jointly with the field can rely on it. Blanket field removal
would therefore risk destroying previous good behavior and is rejected.

Decision
Keep every original and guarded benchmark result unchanged. Do not promote
field-off, inertia, or a score-selected branch. Future work should refit a model
with explicit contact-conditioned or localized interaction fields, or perform
model-class selection using only reserved prefix observations. Such a remedy
has NOT been implemented or validated in this campaign. Local element validity
and low equilibrium residual are necessary checks but cannot certify the correct
physical trajectory.

Records and reproduction
branch-diagnostic.json contains complete per-run settings, checkpoint hashes,
energy/Hessian/J diagnostics, failed iterations and trajectory refinements.
branch-substep-stability.json contains the 16-step static stability trace.
Local prediction arrays and logs remain under
runs/contact-benchmark-v12/branch-diagnostic/ and branch*-diagnostic.log.
The prototype is scripts/contact_benchmark_v12/branch_diagnostic.py.
Example (from repository root):
  OMP_NUM_THREADS=2 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=2 runs/phystwin-env/bin/python \
    scripts/contact_benchmark_v12/branch_diagnostic.py \
    --case single_lift_zebra --start 52 --stop 53 --steps 16 \
    --tag zebra-static-16-stability --inspect-substeps
For full-suffix inertia use --start 46 --stop 66 --steps 32 --inertia.
Use --no-field only for the declared energy ablation and --validation for the
separately fitted validation checkpoint. Do not overwrite benchmark predictions.

Two prototype tests pass: prescribed-input nonmutation and observation isolation,
plus explicit rejection of evaluation targets and invalid integration intervals.
