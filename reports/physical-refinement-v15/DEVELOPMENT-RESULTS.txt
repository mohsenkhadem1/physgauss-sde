Development checkpoint, before independent reserved evaluation

Question: can additional observations improve physical predictions even when
the initial scene already looks plausible? The latent variables are material
heterogeneity, common stiffness scale, fixture/material ratio and attachment
layout. Inputs are tool positions/orientations and RGB-D; one separate track
also observes reaction force and torque after a probe. Predictions include
deformation and reaction on new prescribed motions.

The model keeps a finite weighted set of physical hypotheses. Each hypothesis
uses the same mechanics to generate its geometry, rendering and reactions.
New views or probes reweight hypotheses. The proposed acquisition rule chooses
the next observation by expected reduction of mechanical predictive variance
on three public design motions. Actual test motions are separate.

These are in-sample development diagnostics, not a final test or population
claim. Fixed schedules were deliberately selected on development and are strong
controls. The adaptive method does not beat them here. The limited Monte Carlo
stability audit agreed in all32 comparisons, so this is not just an unstable
fantasy-seed ranking.

The important failure: marginal shape90% coverage falls from about95.7% initially
to6.3% with vision-only acquisition and17.5% with reaction-assisted acquisition.
Thus smaller point error does not imply reliable confidence. We retain this
failure rather than interpreting narrow intervals as physical certainty.
