Physical accuracy beyond visual agreement — completed primary test

The controlled test supports a useful information-acquisition result: additional
position-controlled interactions greatly improve predictions of other motions.
Physical-risk selection beats several frozen controls. A subsequent robustness
check finds that a camera-independent fixed two-bend sequence explains most
reaction-assisted gains and performs better on average. Adaptivity is therefore
not established as necessary. Predictive intervals are severely overconfident.

Test design
Twelve independent simulated objects, four noise repeats per object, two
observation tracks, and four held commands: a new bending axis, mixed axes,
reversed twist and compression with shear. All commands are positions/orientations.
Force/torque are predicted outputs; only the separate VF track measures reactions
after probes. All2688 policy forecasts were committed before scoring. Repeats
are averaged within each object for paired bootstrap intervals.

The method and inputs were frozen before any reserved object was generated.
The same finite physical hypothesis determines deformation, rendering and reaction.
Geometry and appearance are known in this controlled study. This is a synthetic
finite-model experiment, separate from the earlier two-dataset benchmarks.

Interpretation of the comparisons
The two additional views reduce shape error by about19%, but do little for force
and slightly worsen average torque error. Vision-only probing makes shape
prediction very accurate while leaving appreciable force uncertainty/error.
Reaction measurements strongly improve reaction prediction. This matches the
exact common-stiffness ambiguity: identical position-controlled geometry can
produce different forces when all material/fixture stiffnesses scale together.

Secondary same-hypothesis visual/physical comparison
Across all12 worlds at repeat0, the initial highest-weight physical scenes fit
the clean reference image at the observed camera extremely closely: foreground-
union RGB RMSE averages0.000621 on a0–1 scale. Yet those same hypotheses have
0.05033N mean force error on the held motions. After acquisition, V has held-view
RGB error0.00605 and force error0.02961N; VF has slightly larger RGB error0.00737
but much smaller force error0.00458N. Comparable visual agreement therefore does
not determine accurate reactions. These are highest-node-weight scene metrics,
separate from the primary posterior-mean table above, and compare CLEAN renders;
the inference likelihood instead observes noisy64-tile features.
This diagnostic was declared while automated primary scoring was running, before
the assistant inspected numeric results. It is secondary analysis, not part of
the earlier method freeze. Every world is included; no case selection is used.

Post-hoc secondary robustness check: a simpler fixed baseline
The primary fixed schedules were selected separately for each initial camera.
After inspecting the result, we additionally selected one camera-independent
probe pair per track using ONLY the original development outcomes, then scored
its already committed reserved forecasts. This secondary comparator was not
part of the earlier protocol; no new fitting data or test-based pair selection
was used. In VF, the selected constant left-bend/right-bend pair has shape error
0.01055mm, force error0.00341N and combined loss0.000899, all lower than active
selection's0.01204mm,0.00362N and0.001045. Active has two combined-loss losses
and ten ties across the12 worlds. Thus effective probing, rather than reliable
adaptive intelligence, explains most VF benefit. In V, active remains better
than its development-selected global fixed pair in mean shape and combined loss.
See posthoc-global-fixed-probe.txt for the full diagnostic and its timing label.

Across12 worlds, VF physical risk improves combined loss over initial in12/12.
Against fixed-all it wins5, loses1 and ties6; against visual uncertainty it wins5,
loses4 and ties3 (near-tie tolerance1e-12). V improves initial in11/12; its one
worsening case is retained. Random baselines are exact averages of pair errors,
not a lucky random draw or an average forecast.

Uncertainty result — point accuracy improves, calibration fails
Nominal90% marginal shape coverage starts at95.1%. After physical-risk acquisition
it is only5.87% in V and3.66% in VF. These are coordinatewise empirical coverage
rates, not percentages of independent objects. VF force and torque coverage are
68.1% and61.8%. Intervals become narrower than the remaining errors justify.
CRPS improves substantially, showing better average marginal predictive scores,
but that does not rescue calibration. Width, coverage and point error must remain
separate. The risk criterion can be useful as a ranking heuristic without its
internal variance being a trustworthy confidence estimate.

Physical signal and numerical qualification
All48 held object/action states contain nonrigid deformation: its RMS ranges
from0.312 to1.337mm, averaging0.879mm. After removing best rigid motion, the
remaining RMS is18.6–64.5% of total motion, averaging47.9%. This addresses the
earlier single-case0.17% deformation diagnostic with a whole-cohort measurement.
All108 reserved mesh comparisons pass. Held field n4/n6 differences average
3.10micrometres and reach5.95micrometres; final10–12micrometre shape errors
should be interpreted in that numerical-resolution context.

The last gradient posterior refinement failed, and the bounded renderer
supersampling check did not establish feature convergence. Both failures remain
published. There is no claim of a converged continuous posterior, calibrated
physical-camera likelihood, real-world micrometre accuracy, or learned Gaussian
scene reconstruction from this controlled test.

Independent audit and preservation
The pre-evaluation suite passed64 tests. The independent results audit passed
49,714 checks, including raw-array metrics, proper-score subsets, random means,
paired world bootstraps, measurement receipts, numerical checks and preservation.
All421 pinned earlier artifacts remain unchanged. Raw data and forecast weights
stay local; code, derived reports and videos form the publication package.

Where this leaves the research idea
We now have a concrete formulation and an independently tested example of
information improving physical predictions. The useful result is strongest for
probing with reactions. The next scientific obstacle is reliable predictive
uncertainty under numerical/model mismatch. A stronger architecture alone would
not repair that evidence gap. The current numerical and coverage diagnostics
provide specific targets for a subsequent, separately frozen study.
