Physical accuracy beyond visual agreement

Lay explanation

Imagine two soft blocks that look and bend almost identically when a gripper
moves them to a particular position. One can still require twice as much force
as the other. A realistic-looking reconstruction can therefore predict the wrong
interaction. We keep several explanations of the object, predict what each would
do, and use an additional camera view or a controlled movement to distinguish
them. We judge success by predictions of other movements that were not used to
fit or choose the measurement.

An extra view can reveal a hidden attachment. An interaction can reveal internal
material differences that no static view can show. Measuring the resulting
reaction provides information that object position alone may never contain.
However, agreement among our candidate explanations is not automatically reliable
confidence: all candidates may share the same approximation or omit the truth.

Technical formulation

Let theta contain the material-gradient parameter, common stiffness scale,
fixture/material ratio and attachment layout. Let p denote prescribed left/right
tool translations and orientations. For each physical hypothesis solve

  u_theta(p) = argmin_u [elastic_energy_theta(u)
                        + attachment_energy_theta(u,p)].

The elastic model is Q2 Neo-Hookean, with a smooth rest-frame material field and
smooth Robin attachment footprints. Every probe independently resets to the
initial equilibrium. There is no gravity/external load in this controlled study.
The reaction on the controller is the negative derivative of energy with respect
to tool pose, under the solver's declared wrench convention.

The same hypothesis provides both observations and responses:

  visual prediction:  h_v(theta,p,c) = features(render(u_theta(p),camera c));
  mechanical response: z(theta,p) = [deformation, force, torque].

Posterior-mean responses are statistical predictions and need not themselves
satisfy mechanical equilibrium. Same-hypothesis rendering/mechanics comparisons
use individual hypotheses, including the highest-node-weight control.

The renderer uses known geometry/appearance and explicit visibility. It is not
learned Gaussian scene reconstruction. A weighted finite ensemble approximates
physical uncertainty. Given consumed observations D, its weights are

  w_i(D) proportional to w_i(prior) * product_j likelihood(y_j | theta_i,p_j,c_j).

Each update is recomputed from the original prior and each observation exactly
once. The synthetic likelihood acts on64 RGB-D features; the separate reaction
track appends six wrench components only after probes. Unselected actual
measurements are unavailable through the policy request interface.

Define mechanical predictive risk on three PUBLIC DESIGN actions A_d:

  R(D) = average_{p in A_d} (1/d_z) trace Cov_{theta~w(D)}[S z(theta,p)].

S encodes declared engineering units and relative weights: deformation0.5,
force0.25, torque0.25, normalized by1mm,0.1N,0.01Nm and block dimension.
For block b of dimension d_b, S has diagonal sqrt(d_z*w_b/d_b)/unit_b;
d_z=249 is the response-coordinate count per action. Thus the objective is
the weighted sum of the three blockwise mean variances in declared units.
These choices are an acquisition utility, not a universal physical-fidelity score.
Report each physical error separately in its actual units.

For candidate measurement q (view or prescribed position probe), choose

  q* = argmax_q { R(D) - E_{y~model(q,D)} R(D union {(q,y)}) }.

The expectation uses128 common-random-number fantasies. A separate32-comparison
development audit checks sensitivity to256 fantasies and an independent seed.
The policy never reads actual held response outcomes or uses held response poses
as its risk-design bank. A view and a probe each cost one observation packet;
this does not imply equal execution time, energy or hardware cost.

Physical accuracy is evaluated EXTERNALLY to this risk:

  error(D) = loss(predict(D,p_test), simulated_reference(p_test)),

where all four p_test commands differ from fitting, query and design commands.
Compute world-coordinate and rigid-aligned shape errors separately, as well as
force and torque errors. Compare budget0/1/2, best development-selected fixed
schedules, exact random expectations, visual uncertainty and mechanical risk.
Score marginal predictive CRPS, empirical90% coverage and interval width.
Compare predicted risk reduction with realized error reduction, recognizing that
design and response actions differ. Twelve independent worlds are the replication
unit; four noise repeats are averaged within each world before paired bootstrap.

Exact ambiguity control

With geometry, material gradient and fixture ratio fixed and zero external loads,
multiplying both elastic and attachment stiffness by
a common positive factor s multiplies the complete energy by s. Its minimizing
displacement is unchanged, whereas the reaction wrench is multiplied by s.
Thus identical images and prescribed positions cannot identify absolute common
stiffness in this setting. A reaction measurement can distinguish it. This is an
identifiability limit, not a failure to train a larger network.

What this study can establish

Actual improvements on independent response motions establish useful information
acquisition. Beating equally budgeted fixed/random controls would additionally
support the adaptive selection rule; if it does not, we report measurement benefit
without adaptive superiority. Good image fit or small model variance alone proves
neither. Current failed posterior/renderer convergence checks remain explicit, so
this is an exploratory finite-model study, not a calibrated reference posterior.
The acquisition mathematics is established model-based experimental design; any
research contribution must rest on the physical/visual ambiguity formulation,
controlled evaluation and demonstrated behavior, not a claim to invent Bayes'
rule or variance reduction.
