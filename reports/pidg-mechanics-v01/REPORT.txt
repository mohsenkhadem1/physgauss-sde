Connected paper mechanics on PIDG Balls Reaction — completed experiment
=====================================================================
The paper's arbitrary-mesh NeuralFEM adapter is now connected to PIDG's
Gaussian rasterizer and fitted to the public Balls Reaction scene. This is
not the previous material-only regularization experiment. Image gradients
pass through a converged mechanics solve to inferred attachment targets and
material descriptors. Gaussian position, full covariance and radiance frame
follow the mechanical deformation.

What was run
------------
Six separate objects; 13 nodes/20 tetrahedra per object; six Gaussian boundary
footprints per body; 768 appearance Gaussians/body (4,608 total). The existing
learned material network is frozen. The fitting split is 101 RGB images,
validation 11, final test 38. GPU 1 runs initial FEM fitting/replay; GPUs 0 and
2 run appearance and joint image/mechanics fitting. GPU 3 is untouched.

Development retained: v01 reconstruction-affine controls; v02 an inadequate
color grouping; v03 corrected connected-region tracking and SH1; v04 SH3.
The selected v04 used 2,000 appearance updates, 80 joint updates, then a
400-update joint warm restart. Validation selected the checkpoint before
test evaluation. All experiments and unsuccessful candidates remain local.

What the connection includes
----------------------------
Training-only geometry/position inference -> frozen learned bulk energy plus
Gaussian attachments -> implicit equilibrium -> material-bound Gaussians ->
RGB rendering -> image-loss adjoint back to boundary/material parameters.
The implicit boundary adjoint agrees with finite differences; tetrahedral
transport passes affine and covariance checks. No PIDG deformation MLP is
queried at prediction time.

Measured final-test PSNR (dB; larger is better)
---------------------------------------------
Render resolution           PIDG       Material-only      Connected mechanics
320 x 180                   49.54      48.16              25.59
640 x 360                   34.48      34.41              25.50
1600 x 900                  31.64      31.64              25.40
All models in this comparison were trained at 320 x 180. Higher-resolution
rendering is not retraining at the paper's resolution. The authors' published
33.31 dB is not substituted for the matched local baseline.

Connected model at 320 x 180: foreground PSNR 19.81 dB, SSIM 0.9210,
LPIPS-Alex 0.08269. Full per-frame results and other resolutions are in
comparison-audit.json. This model does not beat PIDG's measured rendering
quality and should not replace the paper's previous best benchmark results.

Camera results
--------------
Rendered the first/middle/last test times at yaw offsets -90, -60, -30, 0,
30, 60 and 90 degrees. The full contact sheet contains every requested view.
The connected representation is visibly less fragmented than the original
PIDG/regularizer sweeps, but color/lighting artifacts remain. Coherence is
partly supplied by a six-sphere initialization and explicit volumetric binding;
this experiment does not isolate a material-network contribution to that gain.
New poses have no reference RGB. No novel-camera PSNR or accuracy win is claimed.
The entire public dataset tree was checked (10,227 entries, 11 API pages): no
Blender/mesh scene assets were found for independent ground-truth rendering.

Important scope
---------------
This is the paper's equilibrium adapter, not its coupled transient Q2 adapter.
Initial radius/depth gauge comes from a training-only PIDG reconstruction.
Color-connected image regions infer attachment targets; these are hypotheses,
not actual known grippers. Sphere topology is an explicit scene-specific prior.
Gravity is negligible under unidentified physical scale. No coupled collision
solve or material/force uncertainty calibration is established here. Controls
use the whole fitting sequence, so this is temporal interpolation, not causal
future-motion or new-action forecasting. Positive FEM Jacobians establish
orientation preservation of this mesh, not accuracy of unknown real physics.
