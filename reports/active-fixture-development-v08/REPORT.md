The completed experiment supports useful one-poke adaptation on this fixed development set. With the continuous linear model, adaptive probing reduced held-load prediction RMSE from **41.55 µm before the poke to 23.05 µm**, compared with 25.15 µm for the frozen fixed poke and 30.40 µm for uniform random probing. Adaptive MSE was 16.0% below fixed and 42.5% below random. These are descriptive results for eight fixed objects with four paired noise repeats each, not a population estimate.

The objects combine four off-axis compliant support locations and two interior material stiffnesses (2.5 and 6.5 kPa), with the same fixture stiffness in the generated truths. Four noisy prefix channels precede one scalar poke observation; noise SD is 20 µm. All eight equal-force candidates remain in the comparison. Targets are the 48 displacement coordinates under two separate oblique held loads. The fixed action (5, `probe-x270-y020-minusY-marker`) was frozen from the earlier development screen. Random means the exact average loss over all eight independently reset poke outcomes; it adds no sampling variance from choosing one random action.

Reported uncertainty and actual error can diverge. For finite linear, the fixed poke narrowed RMS SD from 32.16 to 15.44 µm while RMSE increased from 28.01 to 30.14 µm; 11/32 trials had lower variance but higher error. With continuous adaptive probing, RMS SD fell from 54.72 to 29.32 µm and average error also fell, although 7/32 individual trials had the opposite error trend. Finite nonlinear adaptive probing had the same pattern in 6/32 trials. These counts and mean ±2 SD bands are descriptive diagnostics, not nominal coverage or calibration tests.
